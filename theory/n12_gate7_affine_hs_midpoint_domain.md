# Actual HS midpoint as an affine product outer domain

Use paired uniform physical values F_L and F_R on the entire two original
endpoint tubes, not values restricted to their centers. The actual midpoint is

    M = (z_L+z_R)/2 + h*(F(z_L)-F(z_R))/8.

Retain the two independent longitudinal endpoint directions and the two complete
transverse Euclidean balls in the first term. Do not normalize their stored
axes. Split each uniform field enclosure into an exact dyadic midpoint and a
componentwise interval remainder. Their difference, scaled by h/8, supplies an
independent box remainder. Include the rounding enclosure from choosing an
exact raw midpoint center. Unweight only state coordinates; the descriptor
coordinate remains unweighted.

This gives a convex outer domain parameterized by two scalar intervals, two
74-dimensional balls, and a 99-dimensional box: 249 signed directions in 99 raw
coordinates. Treating the field remainders as independent enlarges the image
conservatively. It does not assert that every point in the outer domain is an
attainable physical midpoint.

For a mean-value action derivative with the complete signed directional columns,
sum the absolute bounds for the two longitudinal directions with their radii,
the two transverse Euclidean norms with their radii, and the box-column absolute
bounds. Every column must belong to exactly one group. A common sign cannot be
used to cancel independent endpoint perturbations.

The domain producer binds both paired uniform physical-field artifacts and
their paired eigenpair dependencies, original frames, axes, weights, step and
geometry. The domain must itself reproduce independently. This is input-domain
assembly, not a uniform midpoint eigenpair, response, field or derivative proof.
Those calculations must subsequently hold over this entire outer domain before
they can be applied to all actual midpoint images. No physical quotient or Gate7
closure follows from assembling the domain.
