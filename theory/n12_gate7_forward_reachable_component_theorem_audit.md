# Gate 7 forward-reachable reset-set theorem audit

Let \(\mathcal C_{\rm reset}\) denote the certified complete-child reset
image restricted to the existing positive-lapse, positive-duration domain, and
define

\[
  \operatorname{Reach}^{+}(\mathcal C_{\rm reset})
  =\{\Phi_t(C):C\in\mathcal C_{\rm reset},\ 0\le t<T_{\max}(C)\}.
\]

The semigroup property proves positive invariance: whenever both sides exist,
\(\Phi_s(\Phi_t(C))=\Phi_{s+t}(C)\).  This conclusion needs no scalar
separator.  The certified reset, however, is a local set-valued relation with
a 67-dimensional fixed-event child fiber, not a globally unique map.  Its
global image and reachable union have not been proved connected.  The proved
object should therefore be called the *forward-reachable reset set* unless a
separate connectedness theorem is supplied.

The formal reflection

\[
  \mathcal R(q,v,\ell,\beta)=(q,-v,\ell,-\beta)
\]

is not gauge and does not introduce a second physical time orientation.  It
pairs distinct Cauchy states within the one forward-time domain.  Those facts
do not prove

\[
  \operatorname{Reach}^{+}(\mathcal C_{\rm reset})\cap
  \mathcal R\operatorname{Reach}^{+}(\mathcal C_{\rm reset})=\varnothing.
\]

The reset relation is reflection-equivariant and contains no outgoing
momentum-sign or signed-crossing gate.  Reversibility and uniqueness alone do
not prevent a regular orbit from meeting a reflected Cauchy state.  Excluding
such a meeting requires a preserved barrier, topological separation, or an
equivalent integrated estimate.  The retained separator audit found no such
barrier in the prescribed current inventory.  Thus the assertion that every
transition to a reflected history must cross an existing stopping locus is
not currently a theorem.

On every regular interval with an invertible Euler--Dirac block and a simple
selected eigenline, the existing differential identity may validly be
integrated along each forward-reachable history:

\[
 e_{\rm ord}(\Phi_t(C))-e_{\rm ord}(C)
 =\int_0^t
 \langle\psi,D_H(\Phi_s(C))[V(\Phi_s(C))]\psi\rangle\,ds.
\]

No retained result gives this integrand a global sign or absolute accumulated
bound.  Restricting the identity to a dynamically defined set does not create
one.

Finally, the proposed two-outcome Gate-7 dichotomy is incomplete.  The
maximal-flow theorem classifies finite maximal time but does not prove maximal
time finite.  The exhaustive current alternatives are:

1. a finite terminal-event hit before any existing stop;
2. a finite existing physical/strong/domain, Euler--Dirac, or eigenline stop
   before the event; or
3. an infinite regular forward history on which the event stays nonzero at
   every finite time.

No one alternative is selected.  The valid core of Norman's proposal is the
positive-invariant reachable-set definition and its regular-interval
transport identity.  Reflection disjointness and finite event-or-stop
exhaustion remain open.  Gate 7 therefore still requires a separator-free
integrated event-transport inequality forcing an existing finite outcome, or
global control of \(D(Y)^{-1}b(Y)\) and all existing strong/domain margins
followed by adjudication of the infinite regular-history alternative.
