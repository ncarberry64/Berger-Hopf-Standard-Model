## Mathematical and scope checks that pass

- **Normalization:** with physical volume `2*pi^2*R^3`, the stated factor `sqrt(6/(pi^2*R^3))` gives `integral Q_A Q_B dV = tr(AB)`. A unit Frobenius STF matrix is a unit scalar harmonic. The charged fraction is an L² fraction, not an observed density or a probability assigned to a physical envelope. The manuscript now makes that distinction.
- **Sharp bound:** simultaneous zero value and **full** spherical gradient imply A annihilates P. On W, A is a nonzero tracefree symmetric 2×2 matrix C. The compressed complex structure gives `tr(A J^T A J) = -kappa^2 tr(A^2)`, hence `f2=(1+kappa^2)/2`. Both equality cases and both complex-structure orientation classes are covered. No amplitude or transverse shear orientation is selected.
- **Spectral interpretation:** the manuscript correctly calls `[8+4(a^2-1)f2]/R^2` a Dirichlet Rayleigh quotient. It is generally not an individual eigenvalue away from the round point. It explicitly distinguishes fixed horizontal R from fixed volume and includes the potentially negative fixed-volume derivative. The susceptibility bound is therefore stated with the necessary scale convention.
- **Persistence theorem:** the proof is valid for the explicit free scalar action on the fixed product time–Berger geometry. The manuscript correctly keeps that auxiliary action separate from R1 and from a microscopic BHSM action. It does not silently substitute Berger eigenvalues into an isotropic cosmological perturbation theory. The round exception, isolated nodal times, zero solution and additional operators are stated.
- **Optical kernel:** the six-state bilinear has the correct antisymmetric sign and normalization for the declared first-order nodal tides. The second-order unknown symmetric tidal term drops out of smooth-segment antisymmetry because the baseline Jacobi matrix is scalar. The manuscript correctly limits that argument to an isotropic smooth baseline and does not apply it to a complete second-order map through an order-one local lens. The twist-free Wronskian and common-profile null result are stated correctly.
- **Strong-lens interpretation:** the first-order angular-source Jacobian formula is consistent with the segment maps. The exact common source symmetrizer shows why raw image-dependent antisymmetry is not a source-independent observable. Fixed-physical-source SIS timing, the local lens model and source/distance freedoms are explicitly conditional. - **Numerical caution:** the tiny density-pair coefficient is retained; the larger momentum preparation is identified as a different input. Numerical singular values and equal coordinate amplitudes are not interpreted as physical energy bounds. Sampled auxiliary invertibility/kinetic eigenvalues and weak metric amplitudes are not promoted to universal stability or nonlinear admission. The finite optical ODE remainder is distinguished from unknown nonlinear field corrections.

## Novelty and scientific-value assessment

The sharp charge-fraction formula is a valid, elementary geometric corollary with a useful application: a nonzero degree-two state producing a structurally stationary nodal ray cannot be made Hopf-neutral by choosing a different Hopf orientation. A Hopf-fibre node is entirely charged. This improves the earlier supplied nodal preparation by proving an unavoidable representation constraint and showing the failure of its generic continuation under explicitly split spectral dynamics.

The ingredients individually are established. Lauret's Berger spectrum and ordinary quadratic harmonic geometry precede this work. Scalar-induced path-ordered rotation is established, including the closely overlapping Magi–Lepori–Adamek (2026) Jacobi-map derivation. General incoming/outgoing distance matrices, Fermat relations and equivalent-lens freedom are established. The manuscript gives these proper credit; no first-ever spectrum, rotation mechanism or general lensing principle should be claimed. The directed literature search did not locate this exact constrained fraction, but cannot exclude its appearance elsewhere.

The optical computation is an evaluated conditional R1 state family; it does not show that squashing changes the computed lensing response, select a state amplitude or derive R1 from microscopic BHSM. The link is that the geometric theorem classifies the spatial preparations to which the **separate round** optical model is applied. That is scientifically honest, but less consequential than a derived nonround gravitational/optical response. A referee can reasonably question whether this elementary geometry plus a conditional computation is sufficiently substantial and sufficiently central to strong-lens modeling for the intended venue/special issue.

**Ambition verdict:** this establishes a bounded, independently checkable theoretical/computational result. It does **not** meet the requested major action-to-optics “home-run” ambition. The manuscript itself and RESULT_STATEMENT.md now say so. That assessment does not make the theorem false or the package valueless. Journal fit, originality relative to a broader literature review, scientific significance and eventual acceptance remain uncertain; no acceptance assurance is justified.
