# Could BHSM surface deformations track black holes or other massive objects?

Assessment of Norman's follow-up idea, 21 October 2026. This is a research
hypothesis and decision note, not a demonstrated result of the manuscript.

**Potentially consequential, if a physically derived deformation history
predicts enough linked lensing information to reconstruct an object's mass,
motion or evolving geometry. The present theorem does not establish that.**

## What is already established elsewhere

Lensing can constrain optically dark massive objects. Sahu et al. report
astrometric microlensing of an isolated stellar-mass black hole in
[ApJ 933, 83 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13296). Nightingale et al.
use resolved strong-lens structure to constrain an ultramassive central
black hole in [Abell 1201, MNRAS 521, 3298 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15514).
The latter inference depends on the mass-model assumptions; one alternative
has comparable evidence and is argued against on physical grounds.

Time-dependent black-hole lensing is also an existing theoretical subject.
Pantig's [JCAP 07, 013 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19849v3) derives
imaging and timing responses to a specified quadrupolar ringdown perturbation
of Schwarzschild spacetime. Gralla and Lupsasca's
[PRD 101, 044031 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12873) relates successive
Kerr photon-ring image demagnification, rotation and delay. These are primary
comparisons for the proposed target, not evidence of BHSM surface dynamics.

## What would make the BHSM contribution substantial

The concrete aim would be: given one specified massive source and its
environment, derive the boundary deformation and its metric/phase response,
propagate light, and show what object property can be recovered from the
linked image and delay histories. Agreement with general relativity is
compatible with that aim. A new tractable calculation, a justified inverse
method, or a useful consistency relation can be valuable without requiring
a different gravitational force.

The minimal physical objects are:

1. A defined BHSM boundary, its physically meaningful deformation variables,
   clock and gauge treatment. A coordinate displacement alone is insufficient.
2. A source/background and boundary action giving the normalized causal
   response and evolution of those variables, including mass/spin/environment
   dependence. Initial or driven state freedom must remain explicit.
3. The evaluated metric/phase projection of that same deformation on the
   light-propagation domain. An effective gravitational representation must
   count the contribution once.
4. Joint predictions for image positions, shapes and delays under consistent
   source/lens/distance conditions, with a nuisance model that includes ordinary
   mass structure, source changes, motion and line-of-sight effects.

The ordinary-tracking question and unique-BHSM-identification question are
different. One can derive a useful tracking method even when the response
has an ordinary effective gravitational representation. To infer an
additional surface-state property, that property must survive the allowed
source/lens representations and have a justified physical interpretation.

A precise local test would be

    delta O = K_surface q + N eta + noise,

with K_surface derived from the action/metric/light chain and q evolving by
the same derived dynamics across times. After specifying a noise metric,
only the component of K_surface orthogonal to the allowed nuisance columns
N identifies an additional local state. If that component vanishes, a
nonzero plotted deformation is not a separately inferred surface signal.
Independent per-observable or per-epoch amplitudes would erase the sought
restriction. This is an inference criterion, not a claim that the required
operator has now been evaluated.

## What the current package contributes and what it cannot supply

The exact charge bound restricts a special degree-two nodal preparation.
Its auxiliary-action rigidity explains when that nodal construction can
persist under Berger spectral splitting. The R1 calculation supplies
conditional normalized incoming/outgoing optical responses and timing
relations for supplied states. Those are useful mathematical ingredients.

They do not identify a black-hole envelope, derive its deformation amplitude
or time history, or compute a strong-field near-horizon metric. The SIS
thin-lens experiment cannot be relabeled a photon-ring calculation. Far from
a compact object, a controlled weak-field calculation may be a sensible
first application; near-critical rays need a consistent strong-field
background and ray treatment. Larger mass alone does not determine the
deformation amplitude, variability or identifiability.

**Ambition verdict:** the proposed physical application is a credible place
to seek the major result. A completed normalized dynamics-to-light-to-object
inference result could merit that ambition. Calling the present geometric
bound or a generic lensing variation that result would overstate the evidence.
The exact next dependency is the massive-source boundary-response and metric
projection, not another angular plot or a detector-calibration restart.
