# What this work establishes

The earlier paper chose a geometric disturbance that vanishes along the
central light ray but still stretches the surrounding beam. We have now
proved a restriction on that choice: **at least half of its normalized
spatial power must lie in modes that vary along the Hopf fibres**. If the
central ray follows a Hopf fibre, all of that power must lie in those modes.
Here “charge” is a label for geometric variation, not electric charge.

The restriction holds for every Hopf orientation. It is not obtained by
choosing a favorable numerical amplitude. It also determines the spatial
Dirichlet response when the fibre scale changes while the horizontal radius
is held fixed. That spatial quantity is generally an average over two
eigenvalues, not a new eigenvalue or a measured lensing shift.

For an explicitly specified free scalar on a fixed Berger space, we proved
a second result: once the neutral and charged modes evolve differently, a
nonzero disturbance can preserve that same central node throughout a time
interval only when the nodal ray is a Hopf fibre. The round case is an
essential exception. This free-scalar calculation is not the cosmological
gravity model used for the timing example.

We also reopened the full allowed angular state freedom of the recovered
R1 effective model. Two angular patterns can have different time histories,
so their successive beam distortions need not commute. We calculated the
complete six-by-six response for the resulting light-transfer rotation.
Its mechanism is familiar lensing physics; the new output is the explicit
state-dependent response for the stated model. The two small illustrative
rotations are about 10^-18 and 10^-14 in the declared parallel-screen map,
not forecasts of an observable signal.

The original timing calculation was recovered and independently replayed:
the supplied density-mode coefficient gives a -0.353216-second first-order
change against a 7.753621-day baseline for the specified lens/source
configuration. That coefficient is an input, not an amplitude uniquely
selected by BHSM. The earlier paper and its results are preserved.

## Why it matters, and what remains conditional

The new theorem tells us which geometric ingredients the old nodal lensing
experiment necessarily uses, and when that experiment cannot simply be
carried into a squashed geometry. It removes a freedom that was previously
left implicit. The calculations also show that an apparent rotation can
disappear under one common change of source coordinates in the tidal lens
model. A plotted nonzero coefficient therefore does not identify BHSM.

The result is a valid, independently checkable contribution. **It does not
meet the ambition of a major microscopic BHSM action-to-lensing breakthrough.**
The missing physical object is still an evaluated, consistently normalized
map from the microscopic envelope action and its admissible state/boundary
data to the cosmological metric or optical response. A genuine nonround
cosmological lensing prediction would additionally require its background,
constraints, evolution and ray calculation. We have not filled either gap
by inventing a state or adding another gravitational force.

The new 13-page Galaxies author-review draft includes the proofs, explicit
effective action and reduction, optical consequences, reproducible outputs,
figures, primary-literature comparison and these limitations. Separate
AI-assisted internal reviews checked the mathematics, numerical claims and
scope; they are not external peer review. The exact charge bound is a short
geometric consequence of known structures. Its broader originality,
significance for strong-lens modeling and eventual journal acceptance remain
uncertain. No journal submission or contact with anyone was performed.
