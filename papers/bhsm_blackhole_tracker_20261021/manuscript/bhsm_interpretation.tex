\section{BHSM interpretation of the reconstructed exterior}
\label{sec:bhsm-interpretation}

The ordinary lens fit is part of the BHSM interpretation used here.
It reconstructs the effective gravitational exterior of the compact
configuration: its monopole, location, translation and admitted projected
structure, to the extent identified by the observations. In Norman P.
Carberry's 23 October 2026 clarification, gravitational topography itself
is included in BHSM surface deformation \cite{CarberrySurfaceGeometry2026}.
An observable departure from GR is not required for that interpretation.
The recovered BHSM source records explicitly admit an
Einstein/GHY compact-source sector, in which boundary and conventional
gravitational descriptions represent the same exterior contribution.
This does not uniquely determine the microscopic configuration.

The author's common frame relates excitations, envelopes, encapsulation
and their transitions across scales. It does not require every sector
to share one trajectory, shell geometry or formation mechanism.
Reconstructing supported gravitational geometry and identifying a black
hole are separate inferences. A conditional photometric fit alone does
not validate a gravitational origin; when that origin is supported, the
exterior reconstruction has the stated BHSM surface interpretation
without identifying a microscopic interface or core state.

Let $\mathcal C$ denote a configuration described in the BHSM framework,
and let $W_{\mathcal C}=\Phi_{\mathcal C}+\Psi_{\mathcal C}$ be its
Weyl potential in the stated weak-metric convention. The forward map is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C\longmapsto W_{\mathcal C}
 \longmapsto\psi_{\mathcal C}
 \longmapsto(\boldsymbol\theta_+,\boldsymbol\theta_-,
                  \ln R,\Delta t),
 \label{eq:bhsm-optical-interpretation}
\end{equation}
with the supplied distances, clock, angular frame and source positions.
When a source convention writes
$W_{\rm topo}=(\Phi_{\rm topo}+\Psi_{\rm topo})/2$, its contribution to
our full Weyl potential is $2W_{\rm topo}$; this conversion creates no
second physical response.
The fit constrains the projected response represented by
$\psi_{\mathcal C}$. Two descriptions giving the same projected potential
on the relevant image neighborhoods give the same positions,
magnification determinants and relative Fermat delays. Their likelihood
and fitted track are consequently identical. This is a physical
interpretation of the reconstructed exterior, rather than a demand for
an additional force or a second mass contribution.

The spherical boundary interpretation can be made explicit without
changing the lens equations. In the static Schwarzschild member of the
declared Einstein sector, flat-reference Brown--York energy obeys
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm BY}(r)=\frac{c^4r}{G}
       \left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM_{\rm opt}}{c^2r}}\right),\qquad
 M_{\rm opt}=\frac{E_{\rm BY}}{c^2}
       -\frac{G E_{\rm BY}^2}{2c^6r},\quad
 r>\frac{2GM_{\rm opt}}{c^2}.
 \label{eq:bhsm-boundary-mass-interpretation}
\end{equation}
This is the established gravitational boundary construction
\cite{BrownYork1993}, also recovered in the BHSM source record.
The corresponding principal extrinsic-curvature deficit of the enclosing
sphere, relative to its counterpart in a flat spatial slice, and its
inverse mass relation are
\begin{equation}
 \delta k_g(r)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2GM_{\rm opt}/(c^2r)}}r,\qquad
 M_{\rm opt}=\frac{c^2}{G}
       \left(r^2\delta k_g-\frac12r^3\delta k_g^2\right).
 \label{eq:bhsm-curvature-mass-interpretation}
\end{equation}
Indeed the two principal curvatures of the enclosing sphere in the static
spatial slice are $\sqrt f/r$, compared with $1/r$ in the flat reference.
This is not its intrinsic Gaussian curvature, which is $1/r^2$ in both
cases at the same areal radius.
For fixed declared $r$, let $f=1-2GM_{\rm opt}/(c^2r)$. Local propagation
of the fitted monopole uncertainty gives
$\sigma_{E_{\rm BY}/c^2}=\sigma_M/\sqrt f$ and
$\sigma_{\delta k_g}=G\sigma_M/(c^2r^2\sqrt f)$.
These are derived representations of the same fitted coefficient, not
independent measurements or a fit of the enclosing radius. If software
uses a computational default for $r$, that choice must remain explicit.
The errors are first-order fixed-radius pushforwards, not finite Gaussian
intervals when a mass range reaches the static-branch boundary $f=0$.
The radius-dependent boundary energy and the conserved monopole are
representations of the same spherical solution. Their contributions
are counted once. The fit does not measure a new worldtube radius, and
changing the enclosing radius in this identity is not physical mass drift.
This static local example does not assert a global cosmological embedding
or continue a static boundary observer through a horizon.

\subsection{Distinguishing envelopments through their optical response}
\label{sec:envelopment-comparison}

The organizing BHSM question is which different envelopments can be
distinguished by observations of the hypersurface's gravitational
topography. The author describes this as a hunt on the hypersphere
surface and uses ``frame dragging'' for the spacetime differential
associated with an envelope boundary \cite{CarberryEnvelopmentSearch2026}.
We call that author-defined usage the \emph{BHSM envelope-boundary
differential}. Conventional rotational frame dragging is a separate
mass-current/spin effect; its measurement around Earth
\cite{Everitt2011GravityProbeB} demonstrates that it is not exclusive
to black holes. The current scalar optical fit includes no such spin
measurement. The lens-plane coordinates here describe a projected
spacetime exterior and do not measure a global hypersphere radius or
embedding.

An existing geometric example is the one-principal-curvature reference
deficit $\delta k_g$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bhsm-curvature-mass-interpretation}.
It is half of the two-sphere extrinsic trace deficit at a supplied
static exterior radius. Its relation to the same fitted monopole gives
an explicit differential interpretation without introducing another
force. This flat-reference comparison is not a physical junction jump
or a measured material interface. Existing regular enclosure/state
and matching constructions retain their stated domains.

A standard scalar-sector diagnostic makes the compact/extended
distinction concrete. Assume a stationary, nonrelativistic, weak-field
single thin lens in the Einstein sector, neglect mass-current terms,
and take its projected mass distribution to be axisymmetric
\cite{BartelmannSchneider2001}. For physical impact radius $R>0$,
let $M_c\geq0$ be a central point component, or a constant enclosed
compact component only for rays outside its support. Let
$\Sigma(R)\geq0$ be the regular projected density excluding that
component. Then
\begin{equation}
 M_{2\mathrm D}(<R)=M_c+2\pi\int_0^R\Sigma(s)s\,ds,
 \qquad
 \widehat\alpha(R)=\frac{4G M_{2\mathrm D}(<R)}{c^2R}.
 \label{eq:envelopment-profile-bending}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat\alpha$ is the physical bending-angle magnitude; the
reduced angular deflection also contains the declared lens/source
distance ratio. For $M_{2\mathrm D}>0$ at a differentiable radius,
$dM_{2\mathrm D}/dR=2\pi R\Sigma$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\ln\widehat\alpha}{d\ln R}
 =\frac{2\pi R^2\Sigma(R)}{M_{2\mathrm D}(<R)}-1.
 \label{eq:envelopment-profile-slope}
\end{equation}
Outside compact projected support this slope is $-1$; positive local
projected density makes it greater than $-1$ under these assumptions.
The exterior $-1$ slope is also produced by ordinary finite lenses and
truncated extended distributions, so it identifies neither a black
hole nor a microscopic envelopment type.

Extended, weakly luminous gravitational structure, including a
dark-matter-like interpretation, can therefore motivate a separate
response comparison. Its physical identity additionally requires
light, dynamical and competing-model evidence. At fixed admitted source
and distance conventions, two descriptions with
the same sampled projected potential, relevant derivatives and
relative Fermat differences remain optically degenerate. This is the
same response counted once, with no dark component added again to an
already fitted exterior. Unresolved brightness durations alone do not
supply $R$, $\Sigma$, distance or mass. Equations~
\eqref{eq:envelopment-profile-bending}--\eqref{eq:envelopment-profile-slope}
explain a possible discriminant; this release implements no new
extended-halo classifier or native-data profile inference from them.

The conditional R1 extension supplies a more specific response relation
from its adopted action, frozen background and supplied matching data.
Its scalar-driven Weyl response may be added only as a distinct term
relative to the already represented reference configuration; an envelope
already absorbed into an ordinary metric multipole cannot be added again

their degeneracy must be retained. In an R1 fit the reported mass is the
action's metric/source mass parameter; its relation to a point-exterior
$M_{\rm opt}$ must include the scalar contribution to the total potential.
An ordinary quadrupole is not automatically a normalized R1 boundary

Accordingly a supported mass/motion track is a reconstruction of the
effective BHSM-described optical configuration in the declared sector.
It need not distinguish that interpretation from another theory giving

Those additional inferences require their corresponding source and
