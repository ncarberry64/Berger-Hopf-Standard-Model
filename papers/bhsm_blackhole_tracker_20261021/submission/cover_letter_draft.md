Please consider the research article “Blackhole Tracker: BHSM Boundary Interpretation of Compact-Lens Mass, Motion, and Conditional Response” by Norman P. Carberry for this Special Issue.

The paper presents a reproducible compact-lens modeling workflow with an explicit Berger–Hopf Standard Model (BHSM) boundary interpretation. It fits mass, center and motion from resolved image pairs, geometric flux ratios and delays using the full supplied covariance and nuisance profiling. Its optional, conditional scalar–tensor calculation supplies a normalized exterior boundary response, a uniform sufficient amplitude bound and exact optical nulls and degeneracies. The ordinary exterior potential and its boundary representation are counted once.

The contribution combines a derived exterior-wide admission result with an executable research product: a local app, command line, Python interface, executed examples and replayable analysis bundles. A fixed synthetic ten-year, three-source experiment includes 64 noise realizations, interval-coverage checks and unsuccessful controls. These calculations evaluate the stated model and supplied noise; they do not report a new observed black hole or establish a horizon or a physical microscopic core-transfer law. Previously uncatalogued compact-lens candidates can be assessed when suitable observations and the declared geometry/source/environment assumptions are supplied.

The work fits the call’s interest in new modeling techniques and mass-model comparisons across mass scales. Its derivations, conditional assumptions, exact degeneracies and unsuccessful cases are presented alongside the implementation so that readers can assess both useful tracking and unresolved response outcomes.
