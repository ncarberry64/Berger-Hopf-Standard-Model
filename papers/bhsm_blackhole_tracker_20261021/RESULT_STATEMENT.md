# Result statement: native and joint real-observation validation, 0.2.0rc4

**Blackhole Tracker processes the supplied native observations, preserves provenance, fits a declared unresolved light-curve model, and exports replayable results. These light curves do not measure a black-hole mass.**

The internally frozen pilot processes every one of the 100 selected, previously published OGLE-III events and all 76,344 native numeric measurements. All 109 supplied raw-file hashes verify; the exact 75-long-duration plus 25-control selection is reproduced. No unsuccessful events or documented warnings are replaced. No resolved images, geometric flux ratios, delays, distances or covariance are manufactured.

The rectilinear PSPL model with independent native-magnitude errors is inadequate for 89 events. Eleven are not rejected by the specified chi-square screen; that label alone does not establish scientific adequacy. Only two pass all frozen archival-follow-up conditions:

Both have strong blending; neither has measured angular Einstein radius or microlens parallax here. Long duration can reflect mass, distance or slow relative motion. Every pilot mass, distance and absolute lens motion remains unavailable: **zero mass-supported black-hole candidates and zero new black-hole interpretations**.

Primary alias checks identify 3092 as OGLE-2007-BLG-057 / MOA-2007-BLG-027, already discussed by Yock (2008). 0585 is published catalog source BLG122.6.83113; no specific prior lens-interpretation paper was located by bounded searches, which does not establish novelty. Four pilot events match the later 59-event parallax cohort; 0264/PAR-07 already has a historical remnant interpretation and later revised analysis. Literature masses are not imported as observations. The separate OB110462 known-system benchmark uses 2022 Zenodo version 1.0.0: 61,585 OGLE/MOA photometric rows and 16 HST rows at eight shared visits. All five photometry-only fits converge yet are inadequate and have temporal/seasonal residuals. The HST time export mapping and shared-frame/calibration provenance remain unverified; simply calling its times MJD disagrees with matching exposure metadata. No guessed time shift or repeated filter correction is applied. This retained limitation is not recovery of the published black-hole mass. Newer changed reductions are not substituted for older inputs.

## Separate 2023 joint benchmark: no mass accepted

The new route imports the primary 2023 HST table and corrected OGLE observations with actual exposure-header mapping, while preserving the older 2022 benchmark. The selected joint fit has 12,521 native photometric rows and 21 angular rows at 11 visits. The physical relation is M = theta_E / (kappa pi_E); neither the published mass nor an assumed distance is a fitting datum.

All three fixed candidates fail their declared residual-structure screens: G0 diagonal and G1 marginal-preserving correlation each fail 15 bins; G2 calibrated-marginal correlation fails eight and reaches its 100-day numerical cap. G1's conditional point mass is 7.103286 solar masses, with conditional level-1 crossings 5.98885–8.39883 and level-3.841459 crossings 5.02090–9.91046. These are **not accepted mass intervals**. Both accepted mass fields remain null. A reasonable global scalar chi-square or passing heldout prediction screen cannot override those failures.

All 40 parametric controls, original and continued starts, conditional profiles and whole-night validation scores are retained. The campaign stops under its frozen all-models-fail rule. The measured benchmark has zero accepted black-hole identifications. This does not contradict its established literature interpretation; it limits what this particular implementation has validated. A covariance-robust estimator is a legitimate separate research possibility, but the current data have few, highly influential astrometric visits and measurable photometric dependence; no robust mass support has been certified.
