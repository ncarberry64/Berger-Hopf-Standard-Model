# What the Blackhole Tracker now does

The Blackhole Tracker is now a runnable research tool. Give it resolved positions of two lensed images, their relative magnification, their arrival-time difference and a full error covariance. It can fit a compact dark lens's mass, position and motion, show the inferred track and residuals, and export a reproducible report. A local browser app, command line, Python package and two executed notebooks use the same engine. The fitted exterior has an explicit BHSM interpretation; a departure from ordinary gravity is not required.

Reviewers should use [the BHSM repository's draft PR #470](https://github.com/ncarberry64/Berger-Hopf-Standard-Model/pull/470), branch `codex/norman-core-surface-amendment-20261022`. The product is under `products/blackhole_tracker/` and this paper under `papers/bhsm_blackhole_tracker_20261021/`. [Manuscript-Generation PR #24](https://github.com/ncarberry64/Manuscript-Generation/pull/24) preserves the development history.

The recovered BHSM sources already use an Einstein gravitational sector with its boundary action. In that sector, the mass seen by light can also be written as a property of an enclosing surface. For a static spherical exterior, its curvature deficit is `delta k_g=(1-sqrt(1-2GM/(c²r)))/r`. The source's surface amplitude is `Q_Sigma=GM/r`, and a flat-reference Brown–York boundary energy obeys `M=E_BY/c²-G E_BY²/(2c^6 r)`. These are different representations of the same mass, so the tracker counts their gravitational effect once. The fitted position and motion describe that same optical configuration.

A surface energy changes if we choose a different enclosing radius or reference. That does not make the object's conserved mass change. The radius is a supplied description, not a newly observed surface; the static formulas are used outside the gravitational radius. The [new manuscript section](manuscript/bhsm_interpretation.tex) and [source ledger](provenance/bhsm_boundary_mass_interpretation_sources.json) document this dictionary. It gives ordinary fitting a BHSM physical meaning without asserting that the fit reconstructs the microscopic interior or proves a horizon.

The rc2 update also explains Norman P. Carberry's dated core/surface proposal. Ordinary spacetime calculations describe the surface-side exterior. The proposed common core is expressed through abstract harmonic relationships, without assigning it an internal distance, clock or trajectory. This is an interpretation of the fitted configuration, not something the lens data classify or measure. Existing boundary and interface mathematics is retained; extending it to a terminal loss of surface manifestation still needs a selected matching rule and consistent charge/flux accounting. The app does not fit invented core controls or report unavailable rates as zero.

The result can be a supported track, a weak measurement or a rejected model. That distinction is part of the product. Source positions remain free rather than being handed to the fit as known truth. Distances, the astrometric frame and any observer-parallax factors are supplied calibrations. The image labels follow positive and negative parity, not a brightness ranking.

Version `0.2.0rc2` has been checked as an installed product outside the source checkout. The real browser download can be replayed from the command line; the browser, API, CLI and executed notebook agree exactly on the fitted predictions and share the updated interpretation. An older rc1 replay keeps its scientific status and predictions while explicitly recording the new interpretation and source provenance. The trajectory's rounded year labels have been corrected. These focused checks changed no observation, covariance, uncertainty rule or fitted physics, and did not repeat or replace the earlier 64-draw experiment. The [independent installed review](review/RC2_INSTALLED_USABILITY_REVIEW.md) separates its own checks from the browser/notebook evidence.

A subsequent full usability audit also found and fixed practical input and recovery defects: duplicated fields in uploaded JSON now reach strict rejection, booleans or quoted numbers cannot silently become measurements, and an unavailable server job no longer traps the app in a permanent busy state. The rebuilt installation and 133 product tests pass, with exactly unchanged results for valid input. The app, reports and metadata cite the formal BHSM repository. The review's final addendum keeps the new evidence separate from the older receipts.

We tested the ordinary-lens workflow in a fixed synthetic experiment with three background sources sampled across ten years. Every one of 64 noise realizations completed; none was removed. The reported local 95% intervals covered the true mass and both motion components in 61 of 64 cases. The two position coordinates covered in 59 and 61 cases. Those results pass the fixed small-ensemble check, but the sample is too small to prove universal accuracy. The saved receipt includes bias and scatter, including a noticeable mean offset in one motion coordinate, rather than hiding them.

Two controls check that the tool can return an unsuccessful scientific outcome. A ten-year cosmological example has useful mass information but almost no resolved motion: the tool reports partial identification. A conventional external shear produces a poor, rejected fit when omitted and is recovered when admitted. The product therefore does not automatically turn an unexplained residual into evidence for a new envelope or gravity theory.

The optional R1 extension adds a different question: what extra lensing pattern follows from a specified scalar boundary deformation? One adopted effective action fixes its decaying exterior and metric response. A new uniform bound limits how large the normalized boundary state may be while the retained scalar equations keep their required principal signs throughout the exterior. The corresponding boundary source is paired consistently through the conormal response. This is a useful admissibility rule for the chosen approximation; it does not determine what state a real black hole selects.

The theory also reveals useful blind directions. A leading screened deformation can move images and change their magnifications while leaving the leading two-image delay change zero. A familiar delay-and-flux mass statistic can confuse that response with ordinary shear. Two three-dimensional quadrupole components disappear in the symmetric line-of-sight projection. In the zero-flux harmonic limit, a boundary quadrupole and an ordinary compact quadrupole produce exactly the same potential. Keeping richer image data can preserve some information lost by a single statistic, but cannot distinguish identical exterior potentials.

The earlier broad information experiment remains in the paper for this theoretical purpose. Its cosmological scan would take millions of years of normal motion; it is not the practical product's ten-year test. Large coefficients used only in a formal operator identity are kept outside the physical prediction interface, which rejects the inadmissible state. The optional admitted R1 envelope in the nearby example is far below the supplied noise. No deformation amplitude was enlarged to manufacture a detectable result.

The tool is effective for its validated synthetic inference task as a reconstruction of the BHSM-described gravitational exterior. Its optional R1 extension provides additional conditional response, admission and information-loss tests; that extension is not required to retain the ordinary fit or its BHSM interpretation. It is not an empirical black-hole detection or a claim that all lensing data identify a horizon. Ordinary lens inference and moving-lens software have strong predecessors; the contribution is the specialist workflow together with the stated action-specific results.

Stronger claims still require specific physical inputs: a microscopic state map and a horizon/cosmologically matched solution selecting any additional scalar flux and boundary evolution. These are requirements for those further inferences, not conditions for using the ordinary BHSM-interpreted track. The searched record supplied genuine action and boundary machinery but did not establish those selections in the accessed sources. No journal submission or external contact has occurred.
