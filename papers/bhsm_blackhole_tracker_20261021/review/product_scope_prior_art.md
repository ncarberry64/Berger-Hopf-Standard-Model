# Blackhole Tracker product: scope, prior art and benchmark

Checked 2026-10-21. The substantive product claim should be a bounded, reproducible inference workflow for resolved compact-lens pairs, with covariance-aware nuisance/admission reports and an optional normalized R1 response. It is not a first lens tracker, a general replacement for existing lens-modeling packages, or a horizon detector.

## Official journal fit and submission status

The official *Galaxies* [aims and scope](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/galaxies/about) explicitly includes astronomy, astrophysics, cosmology and astronomical data analysis, welcomes detailed theoretical results and electronic/software calculation supplements, and publishes research articles, communications and technical notes. A validated computational lens-inference product therefore has a plausible journal-level fit. A working dashboard alone is not evidence of a scientific advance or acceptance.

The official [special-issue index](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/galaxies/special_issues) identifies **Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling**, guest editor Stefan Schuldt, and a **15 September 2026** deadline. The indexed page simultaneously labels it open; the listed deadline precedes the present 21 October date. This is inconsistent/possibly stale public status. Do not state that submissions remain open or that an extension exists. The exact call text and current Galaxies author-instructions page could not be retrieved because publisher endpoints returned HTTP 429; no specific unverified issue requirement is asserted here. No editorial contact or submission was made.

Keep the paper's relevance concrete: a reproducible compact-lens inverse problem, uncertainty and degeneracy behavior, numerical validation, an independent baseline comparison, and a downloadable research tool. Theoretical BHSM interpretation should not be required to use its GR mode. Software, synthetic inputs, covariance definitions, seeds, parameter conventions, model restrictions, released version and reproduction commands should accompany the scientific results. These are proposed research-quality requirements, distinguished from a verbatim journal checklist.

## What can be substantive

The complete artifact should take a supplied dataset plus covariance through validation, a constrained moving compact-lens fit, optional same-action exterior response, nuisance profiling, an admission decision and reproducible exports. Its value comes from the demonstrated behavior of that entire chain: correct ordinary-mass recovery, calibrated uncertainties, rejection of nonidentifiable deformation claims, and explicit identification of model mismatch. The normalized R1 transfer adds a specific physically declared response family; it does not select a horizon flux or supply a black-hole classification.

Evidence should include independent-engine or analytic baseline agreement; injected ordinary shear/quadrupole controls; a shear-confounded scalar-estimator example; rank-deficient and too-short-track cases that are admitted as inconclusive; finite-noise recovery and interval coverage; correlation-sensitive covariance tests; and a documented model-error case. Successful recovery of data generated by the identical fit function is an internal integration test, not independent validation. Acceptance-rate and failure/status outputs are part of the scientific result rather than discarded runs.

## Primary reusable synthetic benchmark

**Recommended public reference:** [BAGLE paper Section VII, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03364v1#S7). It gives a synthetic single-lens bulge event, a noise/cadence procedure and input/output parameters. The paper is explicitly CC BY 4.0, so a small attributed benchmark specification may be reused. It is not an observed black-hole dataset and does not include published measured primary-image delays.

Useful input values are t0=56900 MJD, u0=0.30, tE=30 days, thetaE=3 mas, piE,E=0.050, piE,N=0, piS=0.125 mas, source proper motion (10,0) mas/year, zero source reference offset, unblended I=19 source. The published derived input lens values are approximately M=7.4 solar masses, piL=0.275 mas and proper motion (-26.52,0) mas/year. Use the precise primitive parameters to recompute derived truth rather than fit rounded table entries as exact measurements. The displayed RA unit header is inconsistent with its numerical value; use the model API's documented decimal-degree convention when implementing a parallax variant.

The [public fake-data generator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MovingUniverseLab/BAGLE_Microlensing/main/src/bagle/fake_data.py) implements seasonal sampling/noise and returns data/truth dictionaries. The [package metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MovingUniverseLab/BAGLE_Microlensing/main/pyproject.toml) explicitly declares GPL-3.0-or-later, despite the repository also containing an old packaging-template MIT license file. Treat the package as GPL under its explicit metadata. Run it as a credited external verification dependency or preserve the license if code is reused; do not silently copy its source into a differently licensed product.

For this product's **resolved-pair** baseline, call BAGLE's documented `get_resolved_astrometry` and `get_resolved_amplification` at declared epochs, retain east/north and angular units, and export a seeded synthetic table plus a covariance sidecar. If parallax is omitted by the present solver, use and label a no-parallax adaptation; it is not a reproduction of the full parallax benchmark. If delays are added from the standard point-lens law or a second engine, label them as newly generated synthetic delays. This exercises independent positions/fluxes without falsely claiming published delay data. The current artifact must not imply this comparison was executed until a receipt is saved.

An additional independent check can use the BSD-licensed lenstronomy point-mass engine for potential, deflection, Hessian and Fermat delay. This is a fresh cross-engine synthetic benchmark, not a reused observational dataset. Public time-delay lens-modeling challenges are useful future tests but mostly concern extended galaxy lenses rather than a moving point lens; they cannot be treated as equivalent validation without an adapter and model assumptions.

## Paper-ready use-case constraints

The supported use case is a resolved pair from a slowly moving, approximately isolated compact lens, with a declared clock, angular frame, redshifts/distances or distance priors, and geometric flux information. The tool can fit a GR compact mass and track first, then test a conditional exterior response against a declared nuisance family. It should return an estimate with an uncertainty only for admitted parameter directions; otherwise it should report the unresolved combination or a conditional bound.

Measured redshift-free delays determine a redshifted mass, not a rest mass. Angular Einstein scale and physical mass require distance information for conversion. Unresolved centroid astrometry is a different observation operator and must not be ingested as two resolved-image coordinates. Extinction, blending, microlensing, finite source structure, unmodeled orbital/parallax effects, arbitrary environmental matter and source-position freedom can invalidate the restricted fit. A fitted envelope parameter is not evidence of a horizon or the microscopic BHSM mechanism.

The source-free isolated point-lens identities provide a clear analytic control: with outer/brighter image theta+, inner image theta-, and geometric flux ratio R>1, the center is c=(theta+ + sqrt(R) theta-)/(1+sqrt(R)); the angular mass scale is |theta+-c| |theta--c|. Combined with the standard delay/flux mass law these enable an ordinary compact-mass track under the stated assumptions. They are standard point-lens algebra. Correlations of position, flux and delay measurements must propagate through the fit; the derived per-epoch estimates must not be double-counted as independent additional data.

Present the product as a useful specialist research workflow, with an independently validated ordinary-lens mode and transparent conditional-model limits. Its scientific benefit can survive a null or inadmissible R1 result. It must not rely on a software-first claim, an assumed special-issue extension, or acceptance of every BHSM premise.

## Final execution status

The delivered product now includes a local app, CLI, Python API and two saved notebooks with every code cell executed and no recorded execution errors. The ordinary-lens validation includes independent analytic point-mass equations in physical units, evaluated separately from the package's lens helpers; full-covariance and row-order controls; nonlinear recovery and a frozen 64-realization local-interval coverage check; weak-motion and omitted-shear controls; and an exact finite harmonic/compact-quadrupole ambiguity test. The repeated-noise experiment is an internal synthetic-model calibration, not a cross-engine or observational validation, and its finite coverage/bias results do not establish universal interval calibration. The published-parameter stellar example adapts rounded BAGLE Table 2 parameters, uses a chosen synthetic observer ellipse, and generates all resolved positions, fluxes and added delays with this product. **No BAGLE engine comparison, real ephemeris validation or fresh observational validation was executed.** The independent analytic baseline tests and the adapted stellar example are separate forms of evidence. The BAGLE/lenstronomy cross-engine comparisons proposed above remain future work.
