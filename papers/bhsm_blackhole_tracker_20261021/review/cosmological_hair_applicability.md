# Late primary-literature check: cosmological black-hole hair

These sources materially improve the prior-art discussion. They establish methods and examples of cosmological hair, charge selection and stability. The open task must be described as matching **this particular R1 action and rolling background**, not as the absence of such methods in the literature.

## Smulders and Noller (2026)

Their Eq.(1) is the shift-symmetric cubic theory, with coefficients c2 and c3, without the R1 linear potential. Their explicit asymptote is self-accelerating de Sitter; Eq.(5) requires c2<0. Section V allows nonzero scalar current, accretion and consistent slow metric/scalar time dependence, obtaining regular stable solutions. The stability/current relation in Sec.V.4 constrains that specific asymptotic branch. These are substantial constructive predecessors, rather than a general no-go for cosmological hair. [Version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22398v3), [published article](https://doi.org/10.1103/mbsl-cr4q).

For the present R1 action the direct coefficient mapping is c2=+1/2 and c3=-1 when lambda=Lambda3³. Substitution of that canonical sign into their Eq.(5) gives H⁴<0, so their displayed self-accelerating branch is not the R1 background. R1 also contains -V0+mu³T, obeys a sourced current equation rather than exact shift-current conservation, and has a rolling non-de-Sitter background with matter and curvature. No numerical R1 flux follows by substituting its instantaneous v,H into their branch formula. Their strategy may be adapted; applicability and a stable positive-flux branch remain to be evaluated, not ruled out. Changing the canonical sign or dropping the potential to use their solution would change the declared theory.

Metadata: Laurens Smulders and Johannes Noller, *Physical Review D* **114**, 044072 (2026), published 24 August 2026; DOI 10.1103/mbsl-cr4q. This is now a journal article, not merely an unpublished preprint.

## Babichev et al. (2025)

Babichev, Esposito-Farèse, Sawicki and Trombetta study cosmologically induced charges in quadratic/cubic/quintic Horndeski models. They derive large charges from regular cosmological black-hole solutions, and show how accretion and screening suppress observable scalar effects. Parent review of version 2 identifies global branch-point regularity as part of charge selection; horizon regularity alone is insufficient. [Published-version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07882).

This is direct prior for the existence of a physical matching prescription. Its shift-symmetric model family does not directly supply the sourced-current, canonical-plus-linear-potential R1 matching solution. Consequently the tracker must not replace its free flux ratio by one, or by another selected value, without the action/background comparison and a controlled matching calculation.

Metadata: *Physical Review D* **112**, 024043 (2025), DOI 10.1103/bv8z-qbfj; arXiv:2504.07882v2.

## Required manuscript interpretation

Cite both sources near the open matching problem and state that related theories already possess charge-selection and stable quasi-stationary constructions. The missing object is an evaluated horizon-to-cosmology map for the specified R1 action/state, including any required evolution and its linear multipole response. Neither source selects a deformation amplitude or validates the present exterior as a physical black-hole branch. A targeted extension of their method is a positive research route, not evidence that the closure cannot be done.
