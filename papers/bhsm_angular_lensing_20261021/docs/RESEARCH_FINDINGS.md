# What the new BHSM lensing paper establishes

## The completed result

This new paper calculates how one specified angular preparation of the
Berger–Hopf effective cosmology changes both the shape of a light beam and
the arrival-time difference between two strongly lensed images. The two
responses come from the **same initial state**. Their angular pattern and
their dependence on distance are linked by the calculation.

The manuscript is titled **Angular Selection Rules and Time-Delay Relations
for Berger–Hopf Lensing in a Closed Cosmology**. It is a standalone theory
and computation paper for the scope of *Galaxies*, “Strong Gravitational
Lens Modeling.” No submission date is specified.

The calculation has been executed. It is not a proposal to calculate the
effect later, and it does not depend on repairing the earlier quasar image
extraction. Its inputs are mathematical and physical preparations stated in
the paper. No measured quasar light curve, HST image, Gaia position or JWST
detector history is used.

## The idea in ordinary language

The spatial disturbance can have several shapes. Some change the environment
without producing the particular sideways stretching called shear at the
order studied here. Others have a quadrupolar shape: roughly, a stretching
direction paired with a perpendicular squeezing direction.

There is a particularly useful orientation in which the central light ray
passes through a node of that pattern. Along that central ray, the scalar
disturbance and its first gradient vanish. Nearby light rays still feel the
pattern's tidal curvature. This is the familiar distinction between a field
or slope being zero at a point and its curvature being nonzero there.

That preparation allows a clean timing calculation. Put a specified local
lens in the beam and keep its physical strength fixed. Also keep the physical
offset of the source fixed. The calculation then predicts how the
environmental preparation changes the time difference between the two
continued images of that source.

The signature is specific: rotate the source's displacement by 90 degrees
relative to the prepared quadrupole and the first-order delay change reverses
sign. At 45 degrees it vanishes at this order. The source rotation is a set
of controlled theoretical preparations, not a claim that an observed source
has actually rotated.

## A numerical prediction that can be reproduced

The illustrative source is displaced by 0.8 kpc from the reference axis. The
prescribed singular isothermal lens has velocity-dispersion parameter
200 km/s. For the conversion to physical distances and seconds the example
uses H0=67.4 km/s/Mpc. The initial normalized matter-density-mode coefficient
is 0.001; the other five initial coordinates are zero.

At lens redshift 0.3 and source redshift 1.0, the baseline relative delay is
7.753621449 days. The calculated first-order change is

\[
\delta\Delta t=-0.35321609\cos(2\varphi)\ \mathrm{seconds}.
\]

Here the angle is measured from a fixed screen axis of the prepared
quadrupole. The values are therefore:

| Source orientation | First-order delay change |
|---|---:|
| Along the first screen axis, 0 degrees | -0.35321609 seconds |
| Halfway between the axes, 45 degrees | 0 seconds |
| Along the perpendicular axis, 90 degrees | +0.35321609 seconds |

The same preparation gives amplitudes 0.123113, 1.163398 and 2.871297 seconds
in the other reported lens/source redshift configurations. These are
conditional predictions for the stated examples, not astronomical
measurements or a universal size for a BHSM effect.

The coefficient 0.001 multiplies an explicitly normalized spatial harmonic.
It is **not** a statement that the whole universe, or the lens, has a uniform
0.1% density excess. The action evolves the specified six-coordinate state;
the calculation does not choose its physical amplitude from first principles
or fit it to data.

## What is added to the BHSM calculation

First, the work covers the full nine-dimensional spatial `n=2` family rather
than one axial profile. Its observer-centered angular parts have dimensions
1, 3 and 5. Six physical initial coordinates accompany each spatial mode,
giving 54 initial coefficients in the retained sector. The 24 coefficients
in the monopole/dipole part are absent from the first-order **smooth shear**
channel. They are not declared absent from all physics or all timing effects.

Second, the five quadrupole shapes are connected to actual temporal dynamics.
The package calculates the observer-to-lens, observer-to-source and
lens-to-source response rows for all six physical initial coordinates.
Those rows determine the beam distortion and the linked timing derivative.
They cannot be chosen independently for each source while retaining the
paper's shared-state claim.

Third, the paper constructs the exact effective lens potential that expresses
the same retained light transfer in ordinary lens variables. For one source
plane it preserves both the image equations and relative arrival times.
This respects the BHSM interpretation: the effective gravity description
represents the surface contributions already included in the propagation.
No second copy of that contribution should be added afterward.

Fourth, the same state predicts a source-redshift relation. In the worked
example, its quadratic optical coefficient cannot be absorbed into one
fixed physical quadratic deflector at the main lens for both source
redshifts 0.6 and 1.0. The computed tracefree contrast has diagonal amplitude
-9.2059866e-8. This is a restricted model relation. It is not a raw-image
measurement or a proof that every conventional distributed gravitational
description is excluded.

## Why this is a scientific contribution

The contribution is a connected forward calculation that another researcher
can repeat. It starts with a declared effective action, background,
normalized spatial mode and initial state, and ends with linked optical
coefficients and a time-delay prediction. It provides an explicit place to
test a future physical state prescription: substitute that prescription into
the response rows, then confront its joint predictions with an observational
model.

The standard mathematics is credited. Three-sphere harmonic identities,
the spin-two nature of shear and distance-matrix Fermat theory are not
claimed as new discoveries. The paper-specific content is their executed
composition with the retained BHSM/R1 dynamics and the resulting normalized
angular, timing and source-redshift coefficients. The bibliography and
`review/literature_and_claim_review.md` document that distinction.

An optical agreement with an effective lens potential is scientifically
useful here: it gives a precise translation between descriptions of the same
transfer. It also prevents overstating what a good lens fit would tell us
about the physical state.

## What the checks establish

The source hashes were verified and all nine stages of the offline replay
completed. The Green-function optical integral agrees with a separately
integrated Jacobi equation. A further check uses the original angular-ray
equation. The lens solver agrees with the analytic timing derivative, and a
separate SI calculation reproduces the baseline delay. An effective-potential
calculation reproduces the original Fermat values, up to a source-only

Those checks establish consistency and numerical accuracy for the specified
equations. They do not replace a physical derivation of the initial state,
establish observational significance, or bound every omitted higher-order
effect. Finite linear-map completions are used only to test derivatives;
their small higher-order residuals are not new nonlinear predictions.

## What remains a separate physical question

The effective action is a declared conditional realization. A unique
microscopic envelope state and its normalization have not been derived.
The SIS is a prescribed local physical lens, not a solved global galaxy
with its own environmental structural reaction. The complete timing example
uses the special nodal preparation; a generic line of sight also needs
frequency, endpoint and scalar distance responses. The calculation is
linear in the smooth perturbations and paraxial in the ray separations.

For an observational application, one must specify which lens, source and
line-of-sight freedoms are independently constrained. The paper explains
why delay ratios or multiple source redshifts alone do not guarantee
identification. A physically justified common state and independent
constraints are what could make the linked response testable.

These are defined boundaries of the new paper. Its present result is a
completed conditional prediction with reproducible equations, numbers and
checks. Its next physical step is to supply or constrain the common state
that an actual observation would probe.
