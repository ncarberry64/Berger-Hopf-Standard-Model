2. Magnification is an invertible dimensionless geometry coordinate

Write image coordinate x=theta/theta_E along the source-lens line and source
coordinate u=|beta|/theta_E>0. The point-lens equation is u=x-1/x. Its two signed
roots are x_+=(u+sqrt(u^2+4))/2 and x_-=(u-sqrt(u^2+4))/2. Summing absolute image
Jacobian magnifications |1-x_+^(-4)|^(-1)+|1-x_-^(-4)|^(-1) gives

    A(u)=(u^2+2)/(u sqrt(u^2+4)),
    A'(u)=-8/[u^2 (u^2+4)^(3/2)]<0.

As u increases from0 to infinity, A decreases continuously from infinity to1.
Thus A:(0,infinity)->(1,infinity) is one-to-one and onto. Also

    A^2-1=4/[u^2(u^2+4)].

Putting s=sqrt(A^2-1), the positive solution is

    u^2=2(A/s-1)=2/[s(A+s)].                         (G1)

The second form rationalizes the high-A subtraction. If the input is an excess
delta=A-1>0, calculate s=sqrt(delta(delta+2)), A=1+delta. A stable log form is

    log s = [log delta + log(delta+2)]/2,
    log A = log1p(delta),
    log(A+s)=log A+log1p(exp(log s-log A)),
    log u=[log2-log s-log(A+s)]/2.                    (G2)

The research-only inverse implements G2, not the production model. It avoids
forming delta^2 at extremes and avoids computing A-1 when delta is supplied.
If A has already rounded to1, its missing excess cannot be recovered. A=1 has
only u=infinity as a limit; A<1 has no exact positive PSPL inverse. No noisy row
with A<=1 is clipped or removed by this work, and no uncertainty is invented.
These statements are algebraic; a floating-point routine is not a certified
interval enclosure and cannot promise all IEEE extreme combinations.
The inverse sensitivity is du/dA=-u^2(u^2+4)^(3/2)/8. Relative conditioning with
respect to A is (u^2+2)(u^2+4)/8, which diverges toward unit magnification.
Using a separately represented excess avoids that subtraction, but cannot
create a significant measured excess or overcome uncertain baseline/blending.

3. Blended brightness is not the unblended A

For instrument j, let F_bj=F_Sj+F_Bj be positive baseline total flux and
f_j=F_Sj/F_bj. Ordinary nonnegative luminous blending gives0<f_j<=1. Then

    F_j(t)=F_Sj A(u(t))+F_Bj,
    R_j(t)=F_j(t)/F_bj=1+f_j[A(u(t))-1],
    m_j(t)=m_bj-(2.5/ln10) log(1+f_j[A(u(t))-1]).     (G3)

Hence, GIVEN a baseline and source fraction, a model magnitude supplies

    delta(t)=expm1[-(ln10/2.5)(m_j(t)-m_bj)]/f_j,     (G4)

and G1/G2 supplies u(t). This is conditional inversion, not an assertion that
baseline/source fraction are known exactly. They are joint nuisance parameters
in the native route and can be weakly identified by incomplete cadence or
blending. For example a single peak ratio R_p>1 permits a continuum
A_p=1+(R_p-1)/f, u_0=A^(-1)(A_p) as f ranges over(0,1]. A peak alone therefore
does not identify impact parameter. This is an exact peak-only statement;
it is not a claim of an exact universal degeneracy of every complete PSPL curve.

F_Sj and F_Bj exported from a magnitude zero point are relative fluxes. The blend
is unresolved light/calibration context, not an independently measured lens
luminosity or lens-light exclusion. The explicit f>1 sensitivity is a signed
calibration-offset blend, not negative stellar luminosity or evidence of a dark
lens. A is the SUM of unresolved image flux magnifications, not an observed
resolved-image flux ratio.

4. Conditional trajectory information and remaining symmetries

Define tau=(t-t_0)/t_E, t_E>0, u_0>0. The model trajectory can be written

    vector u(t)=Q(tau, u_0), Q in O(2),
    u(t)^2=u_0^2+(t-t_0)^2/t_E^2.                    (G5)

Flux depends only on the norm. Every rotation/reflection Q produces the same
photometric curve; the on-sky direction and sign of the impact parameter remain
unidentified. The code convention reports u_0>0 and t_E>0; it has not measured
those missing signs/orientations. The dimensionless speed is |d vector u/dt|=1/t_E.
If baseline and blending are known and a sufficient noiseless curve is supplied,
the recovered u(t)^2 is a quadratic in native relative time. Its vertex, minimum
and quadratic coefficient yield t_0, u_0^2 and1/t_E^2. Actual finite noisy fits
require rank/multimodality/bound/residual assessment. Parameter names or a local
full-rank Jacobian are not a proof of global identification or calibrated coverage.

t_0 is closest approach and peak of this positive-source model, in the declared
native relative-day convention. t_E is an Einstein-radius crossing TIMESCALE;
it is not a whole-event duration. For u_0<1, u=1 occurs at
t=t_0 +/- t_E sqrt(1-u_0^2); for u_0>1 the path never crosses that circle.
The numeric-origin change t'=t-C, t'_0=t_0-C leaves tau invariant. It is not a
clock-scale, HJD/BJD or heliocentric/barycentric conversion. The source explicitly
leaves undocumented HJD clock scales UNSPECIFIED and excludes precision annual
parallax from that limited combination assumption.

Poor peak/baseline coverage can create approximate additional freedoms. In a
high-magnification segment A(u)=1/u+O(u), the leading flux is F_S/u+F_B.
The transformation u_0->lambda u_0, t_E->t_E/lambda, F_S->lambda F_S leaves
that leading segment invariant and preserves t_eff=u_0 t_E and F_S/u_0.
It is NOT exact for full PSPL magnification or a separately measured baseline;
all transformed u must remain small for the approximation. In far wings,
A-1=2/u^4+O(u^-6); with |tau|>>u_0, the leading excess involves F_S t_E^4.
These limits explain possible correlations, not revised fit results or error bars.

The x_+/x_- formulas give possible dimensionless model image positions after
choosing u. They are not measured resolved image positions, astrometry, flux
ratios or delays. Existing unavailable observation/result fields stay null.

5. Exact physical-scale degeneracy within the declared PSPL domain

For a physical interpretation introduce angular theta_E, relative angular speed
mu_rel, M and relative parallax pi_rel with a consistent observer/frame/time
convention and positive D_L<D_S:

    t_E=theta_E/mu_rel,
    theta_E^2=kappa M pi_rel,
    pi_rel=AU(1/D_L-1/D_S), kappa=4G/(c^2 AU),
    pi_E=pi_rel/theta_E.                              (G6)

Angles must use one consistent unit with the corresponding kappa; no numerical
mas/radian conversion is inferred here. G6 uses the local/Galactic small-angle
distance relation, not an automatic cosmological-distance prescription. Holding
the photometric parameters fixed,
take positive lambda,nu and transform

    theta'_E=lambda theta_E, mu'_rel=lambda mu_rel,
    pi'_rel=nu pi_rel, M'=lambda^2 M/nu,
    pi'_E=(nu/lambda) pi_E.                           (G7)

G6 and t_E are unchanged. Choose fixed D_S and
D'_L=[1/D_S+pi'_rel/AU]^(-1) to realize the changed positive relative parallax.
Every member remaining within the assumed compact/thin-lens/geometric-optics
domain has the same u(t), magnification, blended magnitude predictions and
native-magnitude likelihood. This is a forward-map symmetry, not a selected
population prior. No mass-luminosity or population constraint is introduced into
the free baseline/blend model. It proves that this route cannot identify angular scale,
physical relative proper motion, mass or lens distance from timescale alone.
No numerical t_E/u_0 search-box bound becomes a physical mass/height bound.

With theta_E alone, one obtains a mass-distance relation M pi_rel=theta_E^2/kappa.
With pi_E alone and no theta_E, M=pi_rel/(kappa pi_E^2) remains a distance family.
With BOTH independently informative theta_E and pi_E, M=theta_E/(kappa pi_E)
becomes available under an adequate model; source distance then constrains D_L.
Relative orientation and an absolute source reference frame are additionally
needed for absolute lens motion. Photometric O(2) freedom alone supplies neither.
Source/event catalog coordinates are directions to search, not current lens
coordinates, an observing epoch, distance, radial velocity or propagated track.
