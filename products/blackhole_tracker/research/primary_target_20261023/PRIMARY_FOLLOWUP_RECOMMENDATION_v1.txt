Gaia18ajz is a defensible primary investigation target for the next research phase. Its published annual-parallax analysis, source spectroscopy and lens-light comparisons provide specific hypotheses to test beyond the duration-only information available for the two original photometric follow-up cases. This is a research-priority decision, not a calibrated ranking of black-hole probability or a new discovery. The target was already published as a candidate.

All eight Gaia18ajz near/far, positive/negative-impact and mass-prior alternatives remain in the accompanying JSON. Some have stellar or other lower-mass interpretations. Their published branch weights are conditional on one photometric mode and one prior; those weights are not BH probabilities and should not be pooled across the different scenarios. The published mass, distance and angular-scale summaries remain literature-dependent inferences, separate from the tracker physical quantities that are still unavailable.

Gaia18cbf remains a useful comparison for annual-parallax modelling and prior sensitivity. Its published Table 6 includes substantially lower-mass alternatives, and the paper also retains a possible unresolved binary lens. It is not a clean confirmed-BH control. OGLE3-ULENS-0585 and 3092 remain archival photometric follow-up cases; PAR13 is a previously published, disputed prior-conditional possibility sensitive to source-motion assumptions. None supplies a new accepted engine BH identification.

OB110462 remains the separate published positive BH reference. Independent engine recovery is not yet accepted. The fast, nearby brown-dwarf control OGLE-2007-BLG-224 is a concrete reason that short duration or high apparent speed must not become an automatic BH criterion.

The source review supports keeping Gaia18ajz as the lead research target while preserving the publication hold. Next work needs a frozen native-data/error contract, all model/prior alternatives and failures, known-BH and non-BH controls, and independent angular-scale/source-frame/lens-light constraints. The project can identify and examine a BHSM gravitational-geometry hypothesis before knowing a mass; that does not turn a candidate label into a completed physical identification.
