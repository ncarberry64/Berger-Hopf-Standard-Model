Follow a moving lens.

Test whether measured lens positions are consistent with a straight angular track across different epochs.

A moving track is a lens-investigation tool. Source/event directions are not measured lens positions. A fitted track alone establishes neither microlensing nor a black hole. Fast, nearby, small lensing events can also come from ordinary stars, brown dwarfs and planets.
Import measured lens astrometry, or explore the clearly marked synthetic demonstration.

MEASUREMENTS AND UNCERTAINTIES

Import a track

Use a versioned track JSON with explicit epochs, tangent-plane frame, covariance and measurement provenance. Cross-epoch independence must be documented when only individual covariance blocks are supplied.

Browser limits: 8 MB input, 32 MB replay. Larger supported covariance inputs use the Python/CLI route. Data stay in this local application. Export preserves the submitted dataset, settings, identities, result and a content hash; replay recalculates the track under the installed method.

Position through time

Only supplied lens angular measurements can appear here. The catalog spectrum has no admitted measured lens tracks.

Track evidence

Motion and the adequacy of a straight-line model will appear separately. Distance, physical speed, mass and black-hole identity remain unavailable in this route.

BHSM INTERPRETATION

Track the supported gravitational configuration.

In BHSM, gravitational spacetime geometry is surface topography. If independent observations and an adequate model support lensing, its angular translation describes that same exterior configuration, with one Einstein/GHY optical response counted once.

This kinematic test adds no surface force or mass. It does not identify a microscopic core or a horizon. Parallax, angular lensing scale, source motion, lens-light limits and competing explanations remain separate evidence.

From a line to a useful follow-up

Several events occurring near a moving path can motivate a common-lens hypothesis. Their source directions, peak times, lens-source offsets and measurement errors must be reconciled before treating them as one object's trajectory. Annual parallax, acceleration and source motion can invalidate a straight-line approximation.
