## Result

The full retained action does not contain the requested deterministic map
from the established formation imbalance to a nonzero material scalar.
This is stronger than the earlier observation that a response coefficient
was not selected.

The retained zero-sigma theory has two independent reflections:

\[
R_\sigma:\sigma\mapsto-\sigma,
\qquad
R_J:\zeta\mapsto-\zeta .
\]

Here (R_J) is the exact exchange of the two four-dimensional factors in
the (4+4) Hopf split.  It sends (J\mapsto-J).  It does not perform the
independent scalar reflection (R_\sigma).

Every formation candidate named in the completion directive,

\[
q,\quad {\cal C}_\eta-1,\quad \zeta,\quad q^2\zeta,
\quad \rho_{qs},
\]

is fixed by (R_\sigma).  The oriented candidates are odd under (R_J),
but this does not make them odd under (R_\sigma).

Let (X) denote the complete declared formation state at
\(\sigma=p_\sigma=0\), and suppose an action-owned deterministic response
were \(\sigma=F_\sigma(X)\).  Equivariance requires

\[
F_\sigma(R_\sigma X)=R_\sigma F_\sigma(X).
\]

Since (R_\sigma X=X), this gives

\[
F_\sigma(X)=-F_\sigma(X),
\qquad F_\sigma(X)=0.
\]

This is a function-level fixed-point theorem; it is not limited to linear
response, polynomial actions, or a small-amplitude expansion.
