## Operational interpretation in the tracker

The search first constrains an effective gravitational configuration and asks
which envelopments could produce its optical response. In the author's BHSM
language this is a hunt for gravitational topography of the hypersurface,
described as the hypersphere surface. The actual lens coordinates describe a
projected spacetime exterior in a declared frame. They do not measure a global
hypersphere radius, its embedding or a microscopic core coordinate.

A conditional photometric fit alone does not validate a gravitational origin.
The existing forward chain is configuration -> full Weyl potential
`W = Phi + Psi` -> projected lens potential -> image/flux/delay or unresolved
event observables. The same optical response is counted once. Comparisons of
envelopments require explicit response models and their admission domains.
Descriptions with identical optical observables retain their degeneracy.

An existing illustrative differential is the **one-principal-curvature
reference deficit** `delta k_g` at a supplied exterior radius in the static
Schwarzschild member of the Einstein sector. It is half of the two-sphere
extrinsic trace deficit. Its flat-reference construction and inverse mass

an Israel jump across a physical interface, a discontinuous metric, a measured
material skin or rotational spin. It supplies no second mass or force.

The regular, same-spacetime enclosure/state carrier and matching constructions
retain their stated domains. This note does not replace them with a blanket
claim that no bridge exists. The common core retains the earlier author's

The research question includes whether the observed response distinguishes a
compact configuration from extended gravitational structure, including a
dark-matter-like interpretation. The axisymmetric projected enclosed-mass
profile provides a standard scalar-sector example of such a distinction.
It requires its declared weak-field/thin-lens assumptions and actual information
about the response over radius. An exterior point-mass response can also arise
outside an ordinary finite circular projected mass distribution. It alone identifies neither a
black hole, a microscopic envelopment type nor dark matter.

The current release does not implement or validate a new extended-halo
classifier, measure an envelope-boundary jump, or identify a spin signature.
The source-event spectrum has no admitted measured lens track. Gaia18ajz
remains a previously published provisional investigation target with all eight

black-hole filter. This note supplies no such new map or fitted coefficient.
