# BHSM v15.95: quantum cone-repair gate

The proper classical coefficients obey

\[
 K_E-K_B=1903.527318.
\]

Any quantum correction satisfying

\[
 (K_E+\delta K_E)-(K_B+\delta K_B)=0
\]

must therefore obey, by the triangle inequality,

\[
 \max(|\delta K_E|,|\delta K_B|)
 \geq\frac{K_E-K_B}{2}=951.763659.
\]

But

\[
 \frac{951.763659}{K_B}=1.16999459.
\]

At least one nominal one-loop correction must exceed the entire smaller
classical coefficient. It cannot be treated as a controlled perturbation of
the classical saddle.

The largest absolute Standard Model one-loop coefficient is \(|b|=7\), giving
\(|dK/d\log\mu|\leq0.0886560\). Even granting opposite maximal rates to the
electric and magnetic components would require more than \(1.07\times10^4\)
e-folds. This is not a finite perturbative matching correction on the compact
cycle.

Consequently the next legitimate object is not a fitted gauge counterterm. It
is the full common quantum functional

\[
 \Gamma_q=\Gamma_{\rm cl}
 +\frac12\operatorname{STr}\log_{R_{\rm parent}}P[\Phi;A,H],
\]

followed by the coupled equations

\[
 \frac{\delta\Gamma_q}{\delta\Phi}=0,
 \qquad \mathcal E_{\rm event}[\Phi]=0.
\]

Gauge residues, fermion residues, \(Z_H\), and the canonical Yukawa vertex
must then all be recomputed at that same quantum saddle. A one-loop evaluation
on the unshifted classical orbit or a separate gauge/Yukawa counterterm is not
a BHSM completion.
