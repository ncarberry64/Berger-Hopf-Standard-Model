# Working hypothesis: the inter-ring differential comes from flow

Author decision: take flow as the working source of the inter-ring response; it is not an observational conclusion. Keep ordinary mass counted once and fit neither the flow direction nor its optical amplitude to the third source. The two ring apertures are still projected model constraints, not a measurement of the speed or direction of flow. The present trial tests an azimuth-dependent consequence of flow, not the full origin or strength of the circular excess.

With the prior circular map and ordinary shear retained, compare a coherent inter-ring quadrupole against two smooth trial flows whose *tidal imprint* switches principal axes once in the band. This switch does not imply radial flow reverses. The sign-changing radial weight is the old bump multiplied by 2(z-z_c), where z is fractional radius across the band. Choose z_c=.5 without a fit, or place z_c=.4860 by exactly cancelling the weighted interior quadrupole moment integral k_2(s)/s. The latter centers the switch at 1.724 arcsec and gives zero extra bend at observed B (inside the band inner edge), but leaves the exterior moment integral k_2(s)s³ and therefore some bend at A. The prior coherent model's deflection at A per unit coefficient is 42.24 mas; the midpoint and interior-cancelled versions give 3.47 and 4.65 mas. These are main-plane template values at fixed observed ray positions, not image shifts or physically measured flow amplitudes.

Fit only the early knots for the two quadrupole coefficients and the same ordinary shear/S1 nuisance. No transition radius, outer source coordinates or support choice enters the fit. With no extra inter-ring term B error is 346 mas; coherent tide gives 313 mas and an early RMS of 28.8 mas; midpoint reversal gives 340 mas and early RMS 29.9 mas; interior-cancelled reversal gives 341 mas and early RMS 30.0 mas. Both reversal arms reach the preset coefficient box, so the apparent fit cannot establish a measured transition or a physical amplitude. The coherent arm does better on the held-out position, although it also does not close the gap.

The actual flow interpretation needs a regular-side balance law. For a candidate stored response q and flux F, integrating ∂_t q+div F=S across the inter-ring layer gives dQ_band/dt=F_in−F_out+S_band, with fluxes defined positive radially outward at each edge. That distinguishes accumulation, steady transport, and local generation. To make this predictive in BHSM, specify the transported field and how its flux, storage, and direction enter the nonconformal metric.
