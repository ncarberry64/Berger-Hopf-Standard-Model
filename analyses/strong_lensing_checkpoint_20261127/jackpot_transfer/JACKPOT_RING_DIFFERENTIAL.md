# What changes between Jackpot's first two rings?

Use the same circular-aperture calibration as the private Jackpot runs: 1.397 and 2.070 arcsec, or 5.149 and 7.630 kpc with the working conversion. The first aperture is normalized by [Turner et al. §4.1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08771#S4.SS1.SSS4); the outer main-plane contribution uses their modeled 1.89 arcsec deflection [§5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08771#S5.SS1) and the archived R1 distance conversion, not the entire measured second Einstein radius as if it came from the same lens plane. The first source's separate ordinary deflection is counted once. These are nominal correlated model summaries, not independently measured masses of the annulus or a physical halo.

The annulus adds 1.3562e11 solar masses of equivalent projected mass, or 1.36e3 solar masses per square pc on average. Subtract the frozen ordinary stellar projections and the same central mass once (the compact mass cancels in the interval difference): the nominal inferred excess is 1.003e11 (Salpeter) or 1.161e11 (Chabrier) solar masses of equivalent projected mass. These are alternate model inputs, not a confidence interval. In one common source geometry, the total circular bending scales as M(<R)/R and changes by only -2.8%; the isolated excess changes by +18.1% or +4.2% respectively. Thus a strong change in the relative *composition* does not imply a big jump in the actual total deflection.

An azimuthally symmetric redistribution confined to the annulus with zero added net projected mass, preserving both aperture values, is exactly invisible to both of the observed S3 ray positions in the circular thin-lens calculation: inside the inner edge there is no change to enclosed mass, and outside the whole annulus the enclosed correction is zero. This statement concerns deflections at fixed exterior ray positions, not all counterimages or time delays. It does not rule out a non-axisymmetric moment or a change extending outside the band. The two rays lie at 1.348 and 3.556 arcsec. A sign-coherent fixed-orientation planar m=2 convergence in the annulus obeys an outer/inner **local deflection amplitude** ratio between 0.063 and 0.303, because the ratio is a weighted mean of s^4/(R_in R_out^3) with weights proportional to k_2(s)/s. This does not bound the pair's image shift after lens Jacobians and refitting, nor apply to sign/phase-changing angular loads. The separate annular tide test still misses the held-out B position by 313 mas.

Independent observational lead: [Turner et al. §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08771#S5.SS2) report relatively high velocity dispersion on the major axis at smaller radii and on the minor axis farther out. The nine outer bins at roughly 2 arcsec and beyond were excluded from the preferred fit, and including them worsened the kinematic fit. The study discusses possible nonaxisymmetry; it does not locate a sharp transition or measure an inward flow. Use independently measured position-dependent stellar dispersion/light geometry to constrain the phase and radial transition of a non-axisymmetric template next, rather than selecting them from source 3 or treating a change in the dispersion axis as a map of convergence.
