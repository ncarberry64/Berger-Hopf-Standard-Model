# Jackpot: test of the BHSM archive's linked handoff and pin response

**Result:** the archived relationship between the onset radius and the strength of the distributed pull does not rescue the spherical pin model. This screens a specific reduced law, not BHSM or the more general response of nonsingular structures.

Source: [BHSM galaxy-gravity archive v0.5](https://github.com/ncarberry64/Berger-Hopf-Standard-Model/blob/551ce2c8e4e672538e520629b0d4a8962cbba968/products/blackhole_tracker/bhtracker/theory/bhsm_sources/BHSM_Galaxy_Gravity_Loaded_Schur_Hessian_v0_5.md), §§5–11. Instead of fitting the amplitude and transition of the §9 flow independently, use its linked strength C=M_b(<s)/s, with M_b the fixed spherical deprojection of each stellar inventory plus the same central compact proxy. The §9 derivation is point-style; replacing its mass by the enclosed mass at onset is a declared local extension, not a canonical derivation. Xi=GM_b(<s) R_H/(c² s²); local R1 curvature radius is used consistently with earlier controls. At the gravity-only seam Xi=1 to corrections of order s/R_H. The numerical Xi values needed when the gravity-only seam is relaxed are inverse calibration targets, not an action-derived Schur root.

For projected radius R, sum the conditional §9 contribution C[K(R,s)-K(R,t)] and the prior positive compliant point-pin response q[1-(R/ell)K1(R/ell)]. Here t is a separately declared cutoff (20/50/100 kpc or unbounded local limit), K(R,s)=R arcsin(min(1,R/s))-s+sqrt(max(0,s²-R²)), and K1 is the modified Bessel function. No cutoff is inferred from data and none is a proof of the compact return flow. The unbounded branch is not a global mass model. This is an exploratory superposition with equal weak-field potentials, fixed ordinary deflectors and no extra conventional halo. It is not the full coupled action. In particular, the projection is not evidence for identifying the timelike and optical potentials.

Fit q and ell to the first two nominal aperture residuals, reject negative q, and test stability only in the sense of using the prior regular positive screened operator (not a proof of stability of the full archive). At fixed cutoff, compare gravity-only onset against a separate inverse calibration of s to the central published lensing-equivalent slope 0.96. The slope has a sharp edge discontinuity at s=R0 in this spherical reduction; do not count a jump as a root. Search sampled onsets 0.015 kpc through min(70 kpc,.999 t); absence of a sampled root is not a global exclusion. Fit the same three nuisances using the 14 early-ring knots only, then back-project A and solve for B at a fixed root seed. No position in the third pair selected a candidate parameter. No joint input likelihood is used.

* Gravity-only onset is 18.80 kpc (Salpeter) or 12.73 kpc (Chabrier). For the unbounded optical control the explicitly linked flow produces just 7.7% / 9.7% of the Salpeter inner aperture residuals, with the pin accounting for the rest and leaving total slope 1.162. Across cutoffs the gravity-only Salpeter slope is 1.157–1.162; Chabrier 0.812–0.826. Both miss the [Turner et al. benchmark 0.96 ± 0.02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08771).
* No nominal Salpeter solution with slope 0.96 was recovered in the declared scan. The Chabrier low-radius branch matching 0.96 requires s=0.825–0.939 kpc and Xi≈29.5–32.7, while the second branch requires s=6.26–6.58 kpc and Xi≈2.71–2.92. No extrapolation of the linearized Schur shift is justified at those values, especially the former range. The shortest resulting held-out B error was 307 mas across the Chabrier sensitivities, versus about 38 mas for the Chabrier conventional control. The 76 mas benchmark from the earlier wider susceptibility scan was a different arm with a slope mismatch and is not directly comparable.
* Verified spherical projection by direct integration, the analytic log-radius slope by independent differencing on calibrated rows, matched apertures by forward evaluation, and byte-for-byte replay of the JSON. No fit is ready to transfer or publish.

The archive has a stronger constraint than a freely scaled distributed curve, but its full action still lacks the actual coupling/readout that makes the nonsingular surface response spatially predictive: the resolved loading vector and direct term in the reduced Hessian, and the metric response for light and stars. A next admissible path is to extract a spatial response from those action terms, rather than using image B to select an onset, cutoff, or shape.
