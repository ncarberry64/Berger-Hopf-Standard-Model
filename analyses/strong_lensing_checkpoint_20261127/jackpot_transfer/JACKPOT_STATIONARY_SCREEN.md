# Jackpot stationary core-response screen

**Status:** Exploratory static weak-field model, not a recovered BHSM galaxy action. The archived v11.3 regular attachment is a compatibility condition, and the galaxy-loaded v0.5 Schur Hessian signals a possible quadratic crossing but does not select a lower stationary branch or its optical projection. No stored strain, extra black-hole mass, or new coupling value is inferred here.

## Minimal static class with a predicted exterior

Write the quadratic response about an already chosen settled state as Q[phi] = integral (|grad phi|^2 + ell^2 (Delta phi)^2)/2 + 4 pi G Q phi(0), with fixed decaying metric boundary, in flat 3D local coordinates. This positive static Hessian has no independent exterior slope to fit. The illustrative point coupling Q has mass units and is an effective optical source strength, not the black hole mass. It gives (-Delta + ell^2 Delta^2) phi = -4 pi G Q delta^3 and phi = -G Q (1-exp(-r/ell))/r. A quadratic Hessian about one state does not establish the existence, selection, energy difference or time-dependent stability of the two underlying states. Nor does this calculation derive that stars and light respond to the same potential. For the replay, assume the usual weak thin-lens projection as a test of the candidate optical potential.

The equivalent density is Q exp(-r/ell)/(4 pi ell^2 r). Its surface density is Q K0(R/ell)/(2 pi ell^2); its cylindrical mass is Q F(x), x=R/ell, F(x)=1-x K1(x). Confirmed by integrating spherical shell fractions independently at six radii. At large r the perturbation carries far-field charge Q; positive superpositions cannot meet a strict unchanged-exterior-charge requirement. That condition is not assumed in the primary screen.

## Absolute curvature constraint on any positive sum of these modes

Using xlog=ln(R/R0) and total projected M including fixed stars and a separately counted 1.6e9 compact point, the local lensing curvature is xi = [M_xx - 3 M_x + 2 M0]/[M0 - M_x/2], where xi=2(eta-1). At fixed inner mass M0, rearrange to an affine constraint. Let B0 be the residual at R0, after stars and point mass. Normalize each mode's weight by its share Q F(R0/ell)/B0. The required mean of
C(x) = [(eta-2) x^2 K0(x) - x^3 K1(x)]/F(x)
is minus [Mstar_xx + (-3+xi/2) Mstar_x + (2-xi) M0]/B0. All weights are nonnegative and sum to one. For every ell>0 and eta in [0.93,0.98], C is greater than -2.679. In the central interval [0.01,50] use monotonicity of K0, K1 and F: within [a,b] the conservative upper bound on |C| is [1.07 b^2 K0(a)+b^3 K1(a)]/F(a); 5999 geometric intervals give max 2.67836. Below .01 use F(x)=integral_0^x t K0(t) dt >= x^2 K0(x)/2 and xK1(x)<1 to obtain 2.56362. Above 50 use the Bessel integral representation cosh(t)>=1+t^2/2 and cosh(t)<=exp(t^2/2) to bound K0 by exp(-x) sqrt(pi/(2x)) and K1 by exp(-x) sqrt(pi/(2(x-1))); the resulting polynomial-times-exponential bounds decrease there. The numerical bound uses ordinary special-function evaluation and a safety margin, not interval-certified functions.

With nominal Salpeter stellar inputs and fixed compact proxy, the calibrated target requires C=-3.154 to -2.955 across eta=0.93–0.98. Thus no positive combination in this specified analytic class, no matter how many scales, matches even the inner mass and curvature at these nominal inputs. The second aperture is not needed for this obstruction; a single mode fitted to both aperture masses gives eta=1.157. This is not a statistical exclusion, not a result for all Salpeter-profile uncertainties, and not a claim against positive mass in general. For the alternative fixed Chabrier stellar masses, two scales can match the aperture and curvature targets exactly. The three witnesses fix one scale to a distinct stellar profile length, chosen without S3: 0.066, 0.50, or 6.05 arcsec. Matching the inner observations does not select the exterior among them.

## Third-source test

All models replay the frozen R1 lens geometry and same nuisance bounds, recalculating intermediate centroids and fitting shear and S1 only to the first two rings for the screen. Conventional controls refit their smooth component on the rings and are repeated for each stellar normalization. S2 omitted for the nominal comparison. At Salpeter, the two-aperture one-mode case is 181 mas (272 when aperture-normalized) vs 66 mas for its conventional control. It is diagnostic only since it already fails curvature. The three Chabrier witnesses that also satisfy curvature leave 208–308 mas (297–389 mas when aperture-normalized), vs 18 mas on nominal data for that stellar normalization's conventional control; the different Chabrier control value is a nominal fit result, not evidence favoring Chabrier. Fixed S2 variations do not reverse these comparisons. No new covariance/significance calculation, no S3 fitting, and no ranking of stellar populations. No model here supplies an action-derived galaxy equilibrium to transfer to HE 0435.

The next theoretical discriminator is the actual optical response of the loaded seam, especially whether it has signed or nonlocal components, and whether the outer matching fixes its far-field charge. Adding arbitrary positive screening scales to the minimal operator above cannot cure the nominal Salpeter curvature mismatch. Neither the sign nor the scale of a different coupling should be chosen to make S3 close.
