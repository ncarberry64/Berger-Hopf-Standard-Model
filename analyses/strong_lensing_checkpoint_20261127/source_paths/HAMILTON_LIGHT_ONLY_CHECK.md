# Hamilton’s Object: local cross-copy optical control

No foreground mass information was used. [Griffiths et al. Figure 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.04562#page=12) supplies 7 already identified features in each of three images of a single background galaxy. Initial coordinates were digitized at 2000 px PDF rendering height with approximate angular calibration from the 1″ bar and the stated 2.2 EW expansion. All fifteen overlapping 4-of-6-training splits predict the other two inner knots. The seventh, outer knot is separately predicted from the six inner knots. This is a benchmark on known correspondences, not blind discovery of new source images. Simple mirror means scale, rotation and parity flip, all locally fitted; compare orientation-preserving similarity, translation, and affine.

A→B inner RMS mirror 0.476″ vs shift 1.375″, rotation+scale 1.606″, affine 0.769″. Mirror predicted outer #7 within 0.214″ from the figure positions; comparison 5.205″ shift, 3.278″ rotation+scale, 0.673″ affine. Direction matters: B→A has affine inner RMS 0.341″ vs mirror 0.429″ but mirror better on outer. B→C favors positive determinant local affine: interior 0.145″, outer 0.352″ from figure positions. A negative determinant was observed for all nominal affine AB fits, not assumed; BC is positive for all such fits. Extensive overlap among splits precludes counting as independent lens tests. ±2 rendered-pixel perturbations diagnose rough digitization only, not the astrometric errors.

In separate archive remeasurements of the outer clump in F110W and F160W (MAST 14098 and 13305), the local figure maps were aligned via each image's remeasured nucleus before applying to the independently fitted local #7 centroids. Only the six inner figure positions determined the transformations. Outer clump fitting windows used the known feature identifications; agreement is thus not a blind search or independent discovery; archival bands overlap those used by the published study. A→B mirror misses by 0.193–0.203″, affine 0.760–0.781″ and translation 5.67–5.68″, roughly 1.5–1.6 native IR drizzled pixels for the mirror. B→A mirror 0.403–0.408″. B→C local affine misses by 0.250–0.270″ vs mirror 3.208–3.226″. This is a positive control for optical correspondence, not for a single universal shape, a zero dark matter classification, or a discrimination between BHSM and GR. Next use the same light-only scoring on new systems, rather than treating extra flexibility as a scientific detection.

## Withheld-patch photometry

The F110W/F160W image-A clump patch predicts image B in a disk placed by the six-landmark mirror map; neither B7 centroid nor B7 pixels enter the fit or selection of the target scoring footprint. Relative frame offsets come from the inner nuclei. Figure 12 used F606W/F814W/F110W for marking features, not F160W. The held patch identity was already known and this benchmark is not blind discovery. Rotated / affine geometry controls, no-clump (zero residual after local plane subtraction), and a single circular Gaussian A7 clump transported by the same mirror were scored on the same pixels. With finite resolution, approximate matching smooths the warped source and target with appropriate Gaussian proxies; there was no calibrated non-Gaussian PSF solution. No gain is fit from the held target.

For 18 related choices per filter of cut radius (0.38–0.54″), sky annulus and smoothing: pixel-template mirror improves SSE v no clump by median 43.5% (38.6–50.1%) in F110W, 53.8% (50.1–59.9%) in F160W. Circular clump yields 42.3% and 51.5%, hence fine structure not reliably distinguished from a compact clump (correlation even slightly higher for circular comparator); two bands separately give median predicted/observed flux in the specified held aperture 0.917 and 0.872 without amplitude fitting. These are method sensitivities, not confidence bounds. Keep as a positive control for predicting a bright host feature's rough location and strength, not detailed morphology or a mechanism.
