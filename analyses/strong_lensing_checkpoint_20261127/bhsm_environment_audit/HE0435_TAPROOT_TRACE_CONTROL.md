# Taproots: fixed-source vs fixed-trace control

Norman's proposal: singularities are attachment sites. The relevant question is whether the shared surface locks neighboring structures into one response, not how far to smooth an isolated point.

Fixed source amplitudes in the constant-coefficient linear v0.3 surface equation give exactly additive fields. Their ordinary forces can still bind; there is no additional joint field at fixed source positions and amplitudes. The regular BHSM v11.3 attachment matches incidences and suggests a different idealization: prescribe traces and solve for surface reactions jointly. This is a scalar analogy to its matching condition, not an identification of T with the physical incidence or an imposed condition at a non-spacetime core.

With the sign absorbed into the trace, the scalar response matrix has diagonal a = 1/(4πL) and off-diagonal b = (1−exp(−D/L))/(4πD). Here L=sqrt(B), D is separation, and traces are evaluated on the regular surface side in the idealized point limit. Equal prescribed traces give q_pair/q_alone = 1/(1+b/a). Opposite traces give signed q_pair/q_alone = 1/(1−b/a). Thus anchors can change one another's reactions even in a linear surface problem if the amplitudes are outputs. They do not automatically generate extra pull. In particular equal traces reduce the source strengths, and opposite traces have no net monopole in the identical-anchor case. In canonical flux, both responses still only change the weights at the anchors; neither creates a distributed source between anchors.

Using the projected HE 0435 main galaxy to group centroid distance only as a scale (not as a measured singularity), the two trial smoothing lengths give off-diagonal to self-response ratios about 0.069 and 0.0067. Even the broad length reduces equal-trace strength by only 6.5% in this toy geometry. It is not a constraint on BHSM or a comparison to the halo mass.

For a physical locking test, derive finite-collar transfer, the response of the intervening surface stress to joint attachment, and its metric output from the same action. Compare the joint run with isolated runs at identical ordinary masses and environmental inputs, subtract standard gravity once, and forward-project the residual through both stellar dynamics and lensing. The sign of the cross response, its physical amplitude, and whether it can support a persistent shared envelope all remain open. A change in reactions is not by itself proof of dynamical locking.
