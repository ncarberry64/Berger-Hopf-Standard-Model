# Can the finite stationary Haar strain spread outside the singular boundary?

This extends HE0435_SINGULARITY_CONDITION_FINITE_RESPONSE.md under its source-free, stationary radial assumptions. The result does not impose a fitted horizon drive or derive a gravitational stress tensor.

For event cutoff ε let the trace gap D_ε diverge and the resistance density be dR_ε = ds/A_ε(s). In the stationary minimizer J_ε = D_ε/R_ε(total), and the energy of a region U is
E_ε(U) = J_ε² R_ε(U)/2 = E_ε(total) R_ε(U)/R_ε(total).
The integrated energy in any fixed regular exterior band K with R_ε(K) bounded by R_K and finite total energy bounded by E_max is therefore at most 2 E_max² R_K / D_ε². The extra strain energy due to this radial mode vanishes there. This is local to each bounded-resistance region, not a uniform bound to infinity. With fixed regular Dirichlet data, the field induced across such a band also has vanishing variation because its trace difference is J_ε R_ε(K).

If E_ε(total) -> E_* > 0, the spatial support of the limit is the support of the weak limit of normalized resistance, if it exists, in common comparison coordinates. If the only diverging resistance is near the event and the normalized resistance concentrates at the event, the energy measure tends to E_* times a point or boundary-supported measure, as appropriate in the radial description. Finite energy alone does not imply this concentration: if transverse area degenerates across an extended region, normalized resistance and finite energy can instead remain distributed there. This would not be energy stored in a regular radial exterior. Identifying a physically extended degeneration requires BHSM to derive its geometry and measure; coordinate extent in this toy calculation proves neither proper extent nor a nonzero metric response.

The replay compares two exact piecewise-resistance examples. At D=1000 both have total energy approximately 0.5 and flow approximately 0.001, with only 5e-7 energy in the ordinary band. In the first the strain holds in a shrinking neck; in the second it remains spread over an interval in the comparison coordinate because the entire interval's area tends to zero. They demonstrate that the equations give a precise obstruction in regular regions, not a universal claim of point support. Additional bounded-resistance regions could be appended without changing the asymptotic conclusion. No metric stress or lensing prediction was computed.

Consequence for HE 0435: do not fit this stationary radial mode as an extended halo by changing the event amplitude. The separate dynamically loaded BHSM surface would have to supply an extended response through its coupled metric/shape modes, or supply a physically extended, degenerating envelope with a derived metric response. Metric perturbations need not vanish outside the support of the scalar energy, so this is not a theorem that exterior gravity vanishes. The appropriate next comparison is action variation with respect to the exterior geometry, not another radial fit.
