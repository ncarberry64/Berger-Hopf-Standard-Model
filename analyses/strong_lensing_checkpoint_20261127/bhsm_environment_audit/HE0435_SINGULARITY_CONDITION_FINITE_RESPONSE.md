## Existing boundary and a limiting test

The retained reciprocal attachment is I_W = upsilon I_C on the regular side. The v15.11 terminal set has upsilon = I_W = 0 for bounded I_C. This is a rank-loss condition, not a regular Dirichlet trace or an identified physical horizon. Under the same theorem's Haar variable q_D = -lambda_D log(upsilon), define D = |q_out-q_in| and positive radial resistance R = integral(ds/A). Then regular collar energy obeys E >= D^2/(2R).

In a source-free stationary radial collar the variational reaction is J = A dq_D/ds, constant up to orientation, so the minimizing profile satisfies |J| = D/R and E = J^2 R/2 = D |J|/2. This extends the energy check to the canonical radial reaction. Neither J nor E is a metric observable.

* At fixed positive capacity 1/R, D diverges on approach to the terminal set and E diverges.
* A degenerating sequence can keep E bounded only if R grows at least as fast as D^2, hence |J| <= 2 E_max / D -> 0. The exactly quadratic resistance scaling leaves finite stored collar energy but vanishing current. The spatial distribution, weak limit and exterior gravitational action derivative of that energy are not determined by this scalar calculation.
* Keeping nonzero stationary radial Haar current makes E grow linearly in D even if the geometry degenerates. Changing the ordinary black-hole mass does not fix this limiting relation.

No inference here rules out a nonradial, nonstationary, finite-strain exterior or a new transition law. The conclusion is about the retained regular collar and its stationary conserved radial current, not every possible BHSM channel. It is consistent with the archived limit-point result of zero terminal Green flux. The next distinct test should address bound strain at a transition rather than assuming a constant current flows out of the singularity.

To turn the singularity condition into galaxy-scale optics and stellar dynamics requires computing the finite exterior metric response of a finite-energy transition or distributional layer from the BHSM action, with ordinary compact mass counted once.
