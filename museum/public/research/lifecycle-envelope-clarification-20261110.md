# Norman's envelope clarification - 10 November 2026

**Author interpretation: Norman P. Carberry.** This note paraphrases his later
clarification of the BHSM regime vocabulary. The Museum correction was prepared
on 11 November 2026. It changes explanatory wording, not equations, acceptance
gates, measured values or the scientific status of a result.

## The clarified interpretation

- A propagating excitation is the lowest form of an envelope in Norman's BHSM
  interpretation. "Lowest" names the regime; it does not rank energies.
- An ordinary envelope is distinct from full encapsulation. Full encapsulation
  retains the proposed boundary-dominated meaning at a declared scale and in a
  declared environment.
- Propagating excitations, ordinary envelopes and fully encapsulated
  configurations can all exhibit mass in a given environment in this account.
  The regime name alone does not assign a mass value.

These are author-defined configuration/environment regimes, not an established
mass law or a mandatory sequence through which every particle must pass. A
physical application still needs its action, environment, state, normalization
and observable. Effective inertia, invariant rest mass and a spectral gap are
distinct readouts. This clarification does not assign nonzero invariant rest
mass to every propagating signal or to a vacuum photon, and supplies no new
mass prediction or proof of a physical gap.

## What the Museum wording replaces

The earlier excitation scene said: "Excitation alone does not imply an envelope
or an encapsulated object." Its first distinction conflicts with the later
clarification above. The replacement identifies excitation as the lowest
envelope regime while retaining the distinction from full encapsulation.
"Envelope formation" becomes "Envelope organization" so the next scene does
not imply that the excitation had no envelope at all.
