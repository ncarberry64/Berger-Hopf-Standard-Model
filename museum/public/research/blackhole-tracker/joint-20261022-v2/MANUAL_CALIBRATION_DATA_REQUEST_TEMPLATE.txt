Subject: Public calibration metadata for an auditable OB110462 joint-lensing benchmark

Dear [data/reduction author or archive contact],

We are validating an independent research implementation on the published
OGLE-2011-BLG-0462/MOA-2011-BLG-191 observations. We preserve the corrected OGLE
author file and the Lam & Lu (2023, arXiv:2308.03302v2) HST Table 2 data, with
their calibrations already applied. We do not use a published mass as a datum
or prior. The implementation uses ordinary unresolved point-lens optics within
an explicit BHSM interpretation; it does not infer an interior/core state.

The native-diagonal and two prospectively frozen OU covariance candidates have
failed residual-structure checks, even when global quadratic and some prediction
checks appear satisfactory. We therefore report all masses as conditional and
do not claim that this run independently validates the known-system mass. We
would like to establish whether public calibration evidence can separate noise,
seeing, detector/frame systematics, and the physical lens trajectory.

Could you point us to existing public documentation or shareable tabular metadata
for the following, preferably without requiring redistribution of full images?

OGLE time and reduction provenance:
1. The underlying clock scale of the supplied HJD column (UTC, TT, TDB, or other),
   timestamp definition (exposure start/midpoint/end), applied light-time
   correction, and observatory/time-system metadata.
2. Exact error-renormalization convention and parameters, their scope by detector
   and epoch, and which variance components are included in the supplied errors.
   We will not apply an already included correction a second time.
3. If public: exposure IDs/times, detector/field identifiers, difference flux and
   reference flux with calibration/zero-point covariance, seeing definition,
   airmass, sky/background measures, exposure times, and documented quality flags.
4. Existing matched-brightness nearby-star or off-event diagnostics that assess
   residual covariance and seeing dependence by night/season, including the
   uncertainty of any reference-flux or long-term calibration terms.

HST measurement and relative-frame provenance:
5. The precise effective epochs/weights used for each combined Table 2 position
   and magnitude, and the associated individual exposure IDs. We have mapped the
   calendar visits to 82 FLC primary headers, but do not equate derived equal- or
   duration-weighted midpoints with the original centroid weights.
6. Public per-exposure centroids and uncertainties, or combined two-dimensional
   covariance, including same-visit/cross-filter and transformation correlations.
7. Reference-star selections and frame transformations, applied neighbor/CTE/PSF
   bias corrections with uncertainties, and any shared calibration covariance.
8. The relation of the published relative astrometric frame to absolute source
   parallax/proper motion, including transformation zero points and their errors.
   We currently leave absolute distance and lens motion unavailable.
9. Existing public lens/source flux or contrast limits, their passbands,
   confidence definitions, angular resolution, extinction assumptions and
   contamination treatment. We do not treat all blend light as measured lens flux.

Please also identify the citation, version, license/redistribution terms, and any
documented data issues for each item. If a requested quantity is unavailable or
not defined in the reduction, that explicit answer is useful and will be retained.
We are not asking for a reconstruction of quantities that were not measured.

We can supply a concise residual/diagnostic report and exact file hashes on
request. Any new calibration/data version will be analyzed under a separately
frozen protocol; it will not replace the existing failed campaign retrospectively.

OBSERVATION REQUIREMENTS FOR A FUTURE UNKNOWN-SYSTEM CLAIM

These are requirements to plan with observing specialists, not a current
observing proposal or a promise that a historical event can be recovered.

- A real native light curve with explicit clock/calibration/row provenance,
  enough baseline and peak coverage to constrain blending and annual parallax,
  and independent calibration checks on its noise model.
- Multiple calibrated angular observations that constrain the changing
  microlensing centroid relative to the unlensed source motion. Include
  contemporaneous or archival peak-phase astrometry where available, plus a
  long enough late baseline to distinguish linear motion, parallax and blending.
  A single late image is insufficient to supply a missing peak trajectory.
- Coordinate/frame/covariance and exposure-weight metadata sufficient for a
  defensible joint likelihood, preferably an independently reduced or separately
  calibrated data set to test systematic errors.
- Lens/source photometry or high-resolution contrast limits adequate to test a
  luminous stellar lens and unrelated/source-companion blending. Absolute
  distance or velocity additionally requires a calibrated absolute frame and
  explicit source-parallax/extinction information.
- A frozen selection and adequacy protocol, numerical and injection/null
  controls, retained failed/unresolved cases, competing-mode mass profiles and
  independent sensitivity checks. Long duration alone supplies no black-hole mass.
- Catalog aliases, coordinates, survey event names and prior literature checked
  before describing an interpretation as potentially new. The present pilot's
  0585 and 3092 are archival photometric follow-up cases, not measured BH masses.
