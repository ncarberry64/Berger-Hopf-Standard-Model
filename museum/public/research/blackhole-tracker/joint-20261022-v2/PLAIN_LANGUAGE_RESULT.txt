The software can read and model these native observations and expose where its
physical conclusions fail the declared checks. That is different from proving
that the fitted mass is reliable.

The frozen 100-event photometric pilot retains every case: 89 fail its simple
model screen and 11 are not rejected by that screen. Two long-duration cases,
OGLE3-ULENS-0585 and -3092, remain archival follow-up cases. None has a measured
mass from this pilot. Long duration alone is not a black-hole mass; unidentified
motion, angular scale, parallax, distance and related quantities remain unavailable.
The alias and literature checks do not establish a new black-hole interpretation.

For the known system OGLE-2011-BLG-0462/MOA-2011-BLG-191, the separate 2023 route
uses 12,500 corrected OGLE observations, 21 HST photometric measurements, and
21 two-coordinate HST positions. It preserves all 15,533 original OGLE rows and
the author calibration. It fits angular scale and annual parallax jointly and
can calculate a conditional mass from them. However, all three prospectively
frozen candidates fail the required residual checks. Their conditional point
masses span about 6.55 to 18.06 solar masses, and the last candidate reaches an
artificial noise-timescale cap. No candidate supplies an accepted mass interval.

Passing global fit statistics and the corrected whole-night prediction screen
does not erase these failures. The published object's black-hole interpretation
is prior literature evidence; this run has not independently reproduced a
reliable known-black-hole mass. It therefore cannot establish reliable detection
of previously unknown black holes either. More implementation or a preferred
mass value would not repair that evidential gap.

The current engine produces no persuasive new black-hole interpretation. Its
two archival photometric follow-ups warrant checking existing observations and
calibration, rather than a discovery announcement. Unknown-system identification
would require real adequate angular/parallax data, validated noise/calibration,
independent lens-light constraints, and catalog/literature checks.

The next evidence needed is specific: documented photometric time scales and
variance components; detector/seeing and matched-star calibration diagnostics;
per-exposure astrometric covariances, frame transforms, neighbor corrections,
and effective epoch weights; and independent lens/source flux constraints.
Absolute distances and velocities additionally need an absolute reference frame.
The companion manual request template is prepared for author review and has not
been sent. This fixed campaign stops without tuning another noise family to
force a black-hole identification.

The failed Gaussian noise models do not prove that every statistical estimator
must fail. A separately planned estimator with robust cluster/block uncertainty
is mathematically possible, but still needs justified mean and between-night
dependence assumptions and careful treatment of only 11 HST visits with uneven
information. It has not been fitted or validated here. Whether such a separate
analysis can identify a reliable mass from existing data remains an open research
question; the current outputs do not answer it.

BHSM remains explicit: the ordinary lens fit represents one observable compact-
lens exterior potential, counted once. These observations do not measure a
membrane radius, horizon, interior/core state, or a second BHSM lens potential.
