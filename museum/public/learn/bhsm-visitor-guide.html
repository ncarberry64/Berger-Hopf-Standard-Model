Three minutes in the museum

Complete four-stop museum tour transcript · About three minutes at a relaxed pace. Read it aloud or follow the museum’s recorded Andrew narration, a natural male synthetic voice, with optional device-voice fallback. Pause the animation or use its stage buttons while reading.

01 / Bubble cycle. Start with the rippling surface. In this BHSM illustration, overlapping ripples reinforce into a rogue wave. A crest pinches off into a bubble, which travels with a surrounding pilot-wave pattern. A second bubble arrives and the two collide. The resulting daughter bubbles are unstable: they break apart, pop and settle back into surface ripples. The final spreading pattern represents the proposed return of residual inertia to the surface. This is a conceptual cycle, not a calculated energy transfer, measured decay channel or prediction of physical rates. Pause at any stage to follow the changes.

02 / Seven scales. Read the scale map from bottom to top: hypersphere core; excitations and moiré-like modes; Standard Model particles; atoms and the periodic table; molecules; planets and stars; and cosmic structure. The Standard Model occupies one slice in this broader BHSM picture. The core is a proposed foundation without an assigned ordinary diameter or internal travel time. The slices organize ideas across scales, while the moiré pattern illustrates the proposed organization of modes. Select each slice to see its meaning and evidence status. Each connection still needs its own physical explanation and tests.

03 / Claims & context. Open the Standard Model chart and look at the quarks, leptons, interaction bosons and Higgs. This is the established particle framework used as a reference in the museum. BHSM proposes a geometric interpretation and conditionally reproduces the matter and gauge ledger under stated assumptions. That conditional structural result has a specific scope. Particle masses, interaction probabilities and cosmological responses each require further physical constructions and independent checks. The conventional minimal Standard Model also leaves neutrino masses outside its original field content. Use the exhibit labels to keep established references, conditional results and proposed interpretations distinct.

04 / Evidence & tests. Finish at the evidence guide. Choose one claim and follow its source. Look for what was assumed, what was calculated, the result’s stated scope and the observations used for comparison. A physical prediction must be fixed independently of the measured answer it will face. Historical numerical screens and certified calculations on specified backgrounds retain those classifications. Useful questions include: which observable follows from the construction, what could falsify it, and can another researcher reproduce the result? Follow the current research record for the unresolved steps. The museum invites careful exploration, independent checking and specific criticism.

Explore together / 20 minutes

Separate the picture from the claim.

Work in pairs or groups of three. Use the live museum if available; the transcript and claim cards in this guide also support the complete activity offline.

Learning objectives

  • Place the Standard Model within the seven-slice conceptual map.
  • Distinguish reference physics, a BHSM proposal and an illustrative choice.
  • Identify evidence a specific claim would need.

Materials & setup

One guide per group, pencils and an optional museum screen. Assign a reader, an evidence checker and a reporter; pairs can share roles. Print the worksheet separately for individual use.

Keep the evidence categories visible

Conventional reference: the Standard Model organizes fundamental particles and the electromagnetic, weak and strong interactions. It does not include gravity. Its minimal form does not supply neutrino masses. The particle chart introduces this established framework; consult CERN’s overview for context.

BHSM proposal or conditional result: the core-to-field interpretation and moiré analogy remain proposed connections. Reproducing the Standard Model matter and gauge ledger under specified assumptions is a conditional structural result; it is not a first-principles derivation from geometry alone.

Presentation choice: ripple height, bubble shape, pilot-wave patterns, symbolic daughter bubbles and slice spacing help explain ideas. The return to ripples depicts the proposed return of residual inertia; no inertia or energy transfer is calculated. Animation timing supplies no measured lifetime or decay probability.

Three discussion questions

  1. What can the scale atlas help you understand, and what cannot be concluded from the arrangement of its slices?
  2. If a calculation agrees with a measurement, what would you check before calling it a prediction?
  3. Choose one proposed connection. What would need to be specified before an observation could test it?

Make the evidence traceable

One claim. Six checks.

Name / group: Date:

Choose a claim card below or copy one precise claim from an exhibit. “Not supplied here” is a valid answer when information is missing. Do not invent a value or an experiment to fill a blank.

CARD A / PARTICLE CHARTThe Standard Model organizes quarks, leptons, interaction bosons and the Higgs.
CARD B / SCALE ATLASBHSM proposes a common core connected to excitations on spacetime fields.
CARD C / BUBBLE CYCLEThe welcome animation shows unstable daughter bubbles popping back into surface ripples.

  1. State the claim exactly.

    Which card or exhibit? What is the claim about, and what does it leave out?

  2. Classify the claim.

    Reference physics / BHSM proposal / conditional result / historical numerical screen / presentation choice. Explain your selection; categories can overlap.

  3. Record the source and scope.

    Title, author or organization, date or revision, and applicable system or assumptions. For offline work, record this guide’s edition and the named source to check later.

  4. Identify the actual support.

    Observation, measurement, derivation, numerical calculation, or explanatory picture? Does the support establish this claim or only a narrower statement?

  5. Name what is missing and the next check.

    Which definition, calculation or independent measurement is needed? What outcome could challenge the claim once it is specified? A picture alone may have no physical prediction to test.

  6. Write a careful conclusion.

    “The source supports … within … . It does not yet establish … .”

Teacher & facilitator notes

Reward precision, not agreement.

Assess whether learners connect a claim to appropriate support. They do not need to endorse or reject the full BHSM framework to complete the activity well.

Card A · Established reference framework
The grouping is part of the conventional Standard Model. CERN is a suitable introductory source; the museum chart is a summary. This does not establish that BHSM derives the particles. A strong answer separates the classification from the proposed connection to the lower slices.
Card B · Attributed BHSM proposal
The core and its relation to fields are proposed interpretations. The 22 October amendment specifies no ordinary distance, duration or trajectory inside the core. The diagram supplies no complete physical law or calibrated observable for the connection. A useful next step names the missing correspondence and a resulting measurable quantity, without claiming those have already been derived.
Card C · A statement about an illustration
The animation confirms what is depicted, not a physical decay channel or a measured transfer. The final ripples represent the proposed return of residual inertia; no inertia or energy value is computed. A physical construction would need identified fields, particles and interactions, conservation accounting and quantitative evolution. A pilot-wave pattern and popping bubbles alone establish none of these.

Reading the family paths: I, II and III cross the proposed mode layer into the three Standard Model fermion generations. The ordinary-atom route follows generation I’s up/down quarks and electron; heavier charged members of II and III are not ordinary atomic constituents. Neutrinos remain in all three families; exotic atoms are outside this diagram. Above atoms, the fork illustrates molecular mirror-image handedness for chiral molecules, distinct from particle generations and particle chirality. Not every molecule is chiral. “Left” and “right” are visual labels, not assigned R/S configurations. Ask: “What changes in meaning at the fork?” See IUPAC’s definition of chirality.

Discussion guidance

Reading the charge threads: plus and minus mean electric charge, not spin or molecular handedness. At the particle slice, the routes skip neutrinos and other electrically neutral particles. At larger scales, they track charged constituents such as nuclei and electrons, or charged plasma; they do not assign net charge to an entire atom, molecule, planet or universe. Ask: “How can a neutral object contain charged parts?”

  1. Atlas: it compares levels of organization and locates the Standard Model. It cannot establish a physical shell structure, chronology or derivation across every scale.
  2. Numerical agreement: ask whether measured data helped choose parameters, modes or normalization; check declared assumptions, uncertainties, scope and an independent comparison. Agreement alone cannot settle the origin of the answer.
  3. A testable connection: look for a defined system, a calculable observable and a comparison that could disagree. Where these are missing, the correct conclusion is that a test has not yet been specified.

A successful worksheet states a bounded claim, names a traceable source, distinguishes support from illustration, and identifies a concrete unresolved question.
