Then S_U^2=I and Tr S_U=0. Each transmitted rank-N sector has N principal
Dirichlet and N principal Robin components. The opposite image contributions

Gilkey/Kirsten/Vassilevich, Theorem 2.3,

5. Conditional native-length closure, including membrane inertia

Suppose the common-length equality is established on one selected physical
branch. Put p=m/2. The exact coefficient equation is
  F(gamma,X)=gamma-alpha [r(gamma,X)/i(gamma,X)]^p=0,
on gamma>0, i>0, r>0. The mode, pairing, field, geometry and domain are X.
This is a coupled equation; the native cutoff is not an extra independently
adjustable scale. Positivity applies to i and TOTAL r. J need not be positive.

If one artificially freezes i,j,h as positive constants and r=gamma j+h,
set y=j gamma/h and beta=alpha j h^(p-1)/i^p. Then
  y/(1+y)^p=beta.
For p>1 its unique maximum is at y*=1/(p-1), with
  beta_crit=(p-1)^(p-1)/p^p.
There are two, one double, or no positive roots for beta below, equal to, or
above this maximum. This is a correct FROZEN-I algebraic control only.

Even at fixed background/mode, the membrane has direct Legendre inertia.
For its area coefficient a_Sigma=-N rho sqrt(1-U^2), rho=A^3 B^3,
  U_sdot=C/(N C_star),
  partial_sdot^2 a_Sigma
    =rho C^2/[N C_star^2 (1-U^2)^(3/2)] >0.
The actual new real-state area packet gives this coordinate Hessian per gamma
as 9.292773856586832 initially and 9.292303221887881 at the stage. These are
coordinate contacts, not the selected physical mode contraction i_surface.
They prove that deleting the direct gamma derivative of inertia is generally
unjustified before mode or background motion is even considered.
One may instead enforce the preferred normalization <psi,I psi>=1 at every
gamma by rescaling the mode. Its mode derivative then cancels this direct
inertia derivative, while changing the contractions j and h. This is a valid
normalization change, not permission to freeze i,j,h simultaneously. The
ratio i/r and the full closure remain invariant under common mode rescaling.

A more faithful frozen-base model is
  i=i0+k gamma, r=h+j gamma,
with i0,k,h,j>0. Let d=k h/(j i0), y=j gamma/h,
beta=alpha j h^(p-1)/i0^p. Its equation is
  f_d(y)=y[(1+d y)/(1+y)]^p=beta.
Now f_d(0)=0 and f_d(infinity)=infinity, so at least one positive root exists
for every beta>0. Its critical-point numerator is
  d y^2+[(1-p)+(1+p)d]y+1.
For p>1 two positive stationary points occur precisely when
  0<d<[(p-1)/(p+1)]^2.
They give one or three positive roots as beta crosses the local minimum and
maximum, with a double and a simple root at either critical value. Otherwise

The k=0 model is a singular limit in which a large positive root can escape
to infinity. Its no-root classification is not the physical membrane result.

Full branch motion adds further terms and can change this frozen-base root
classification. It is not an existence or uniqueness theorem for BHSM.
